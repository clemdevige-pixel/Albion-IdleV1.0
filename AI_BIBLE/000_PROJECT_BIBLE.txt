# 1. PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT

This document defines the current identity, direction and non-negotiable rules of the project.

It is the highest-level source of truth for the development of the game.

All future design documents, technical specifications, game data and implementations must remain compatible with this Project Bible.

When another document contradicts this document:

1. The Project Bible takes priority.
2. The contradiction must be reported.
3. Claude must not silently choose an interpretation.
4. The Game Director must validate any structural change.

This document does not contain final balancing values.

Values such as damage, experience requirements, drop rates, crafting costs, stage counts and durability losses will be defined in dedicated documents and game data.

---

# 2. PROJECT IDENTITY

## 2.1 Game type

**LOCKED**

Albion Idle is a:

> Single-player idle RPG inspired by the progression philosophy and universe structure of Albion Online.

It is not an economic simulator.

It is not a multiplayer MMO.

It is not a fully automated game without meaningful player decisions.

The player controls one persistent hero who explores increasingly dangerous regions, gathers resources, crafts equipment, improves their masteries and prepares builds capable of defeating stronger enemies.

---

## 2.2 Core fantasy

**LOCKED**

> Explore Albion, gather its resources, forge your own equipment and become powerful enough to conquer its most dangerous regions.

The hero must always remain at the center of the experience.

Every major system must ultimately contribute to the progression, preparation or power of the hero.

---

## 2.3 Fundamental experience

The intended experience is:

> A role-playing game that continues progressing while the player is absent.

The idle component supports the RPG.

The idle component must not replace the RPG.

The player should regularly make meaningful decisions involving:

* equipment;
* weapons;
* abilities;
* passive effects;
* consumables;
* farming locations;
* risk;
* preparation;
* progression priorities.

The character may act automatically, but the player remains responsible for strategy and preparation.

---

# 3. DESIGN VALUES

## 3.1 Progression

**LOCKED**

The player should regularly feel that something is improving, unlocking or becoming more efficient.

Long-term power progression may be slow.

Short-term rewards must remain frequent.

Examples of frequent rewards include:

* mastery levels;
* new recipes;
* new resources;
* new equipment;
* new weapon abilities;
* passive ability unlocks;
* new monsters;
* new stages;
* new buildings;
* worker improvements;
* boss victories;
* access to new zones.

The macro progression is slow.

The micro progression is frequent.

---

## 3.2 Preparation

**LOCKED**

Progression must not depend only on waiting.

The player must prepare before overcoming major challenges.

Preparation may involve:

* crafting stronger equipment;
* improving equipment quality;
* raising relevant masteries;
* changing weapon;
* changing active abilities;
* choosing a different passive;
* changing armor;
* using appropriate food;
* using appropriate potions;
* repairing equipment;
* selecting a better farming strategy.

A new region must be earned through preparation, not merely through accumulated playtime.

---

## 3.3 Optimization

**LOCKED**

Thinking should usually be more efficient than blindly grinding.

Grinding remains an important part of the game, but it must not always be the only answer to a progression wall.

The player should sometimes progress faster by:

* changing strategy;
* changing build;
* targeting a different resource;
* improving a specific mastery;
* using a better consumable;
* adapting to an enemy mechanic.

The game must reward understanding and adaptation without requiring excessive mechanical complexity.

---

# 4. DESIGN PILLARS

## 4.1 The hero is the center

**LOCKED**

All major systems exist to support the hero.

This includes:

* gathering;
* refining;
* crafting;
* workers;
* the island;
* the economy;
* buildings;
* consumables;
* exploration.

These systems must never become disconnected management games that overshadow the character.

---

## 4.2 Combat and economy have equal importance

**LOCKED**

The target balance is approximately:

* 50% combat and adventure;
* 50% gathering, refining, crafting and economic preparation.

Neither half should function independently.

Combat unlocks access to better resources and content.

Economic progression creates the equipment and supplies required for stronger combat.

---

## 4.3 Simple statistics, meaningful choices

**LOCKED**

The game should use a limited number of understandable base statistics.

Examples may include:

* Health;
* Attack;
* Defense;
* Attack Speed.

The final stat list will be defined later.

The game must avoid unnecessary traditional RPG attributes such as Strength, Dexterity and Intelligence unless a future validated design requires them.

Depth should come primarily from:

* equipment;
* weapon abilities;
* passive effects;
* masteries;
* consumables;
* enemy mechanics;
* build synergies.

---

## 4.4 Data-driven design

**LOCKED**

Gameplay content and balancing values must not be hardcoded when they can reasonably be represented as data.

The following should be data-driven:

* items;
* equipment;
* resources;
* recipes;
* monsters;
* bosses;
* stages;
* zones;
* loot tables;
* abilities;
* passive effects;
* mastery requirements;
* experience curves;
* damage values;
* durability values;
* buildings;
* workers;
* events.

Game systems should interpret game data rather than embed content directly into code.

---

## 4.5 Single-player first

**LOCKED**

The complete game must be playable without:

* other players;
* guilds;
* trading;
* PvP;
* player markets;
* social dependencies.

Possible multiplayer features may be considered later but must not be required by the core architecture or progression.

---

# 5. PLAYER CHARACTER

## 5.1 One persistent character

**LOCKED**

The player controls one character.

There are no alternate characters.

There are no traditional classes.

The character develops according to the activities, weapons and equipment used.

---

## 5.2 No global character level

**LOCKED**

The character does not have a traditional visible global level.

Progression is represented through specific masteries.

Examples include:

* Combat Mastery;
* Sword Mastery;
* Bow Mastery;
* Mining Mastery;
* Skinning Mastery;
* Fishing Mastery;
* Plate Armor Mastery;
* Leather Armor Mastery;
* Refining Masteries;
* Crafting Masteries.

The exact mastery structure will be defined in the character progression documentation.

---

## 5.3 Use-based progression

**LOCKED**

The character improves through what they practice.

Examples:

* using a sword improves sword-related progression;
* mining improves mining progression;
* crafting swords improves the appropriate crafting mastery;
* wearing a type of armor improves its related progression if validated by the final mastery design.

No traditional class selection permanently restricts the player.

---

## 5.4 Hybrid power progression

**LOCKED**

Character power follows a hybrid model:

* permanent progression comes from masteries and long-term unlocks;
* equipment provides the majority of immediate combat power.

A highly mastered character using weak equipment should not automatically overpower content intended for stronger tiers.

A well-equipped character with insufficient relevant progression may also encounter limitations.

Both long-term progression and equipment preparation matter.

---

# 6. CORE GAMEPLAY LOOP

## 6.1 Primary loop

**LOCKED**

The central loop is:

Explore
↓
Gather
↓
Refine
↓
Craft
↓
Equip
↓
Fight
↓
Loot
↓
Unlock new stages and regions
↓
Repeat

Not every activity must occur during every individual session, but all major systems must support this loop.

---

## 6.2 Adventure is the objective

**LOCKED**

The economy is a means.

Adventure and progression are the objectives.

The player does not gather resources merely to accumulate wealth.

The player gathers resources primarily to:

* craft equipment;
* craft tools;
* produce consumables;
* improve buildings;
* prepare for stronger content.

---

## 6.3 Combat as proof of readiness

**LOCKED**

Combat does not only generate resources.

Combat proves whether the player is ready to progress.

Bosses and important stages act as examinations of:

* damage;
* survivability;
* preparation;
* build quality;
* mastery investment;
* strategic understanding.

---

# 7. COMBAT PHILOSOPHY

## 7.1 Semi-idle combat

**LOCKED**

Combat is semi-idle.

Basic attacks and standard combat behavior operate automatically.

The player may intervene through limited strategic actions.

Possible interventions include:

* activating abilities;
* using a potion;
* triggering a temporary effect;
* changing preparation before the next attempt.

Manual intervention should be useful but should not require constant attention.

The game must remain playable as an idle experience.

---

## 7.2 Weapon-based abilities

**LOCKED**

Abilities are primarily defined by the equipped weapon.

The character does not use a large universal spellbook disconnected from equipment.

Changing weapon changes the available combat identity and strategic possibilities.

---

## 7.3 Ability structure

**LOCKED AS CURRENT TARGET**

The current target for a fully developed weapon loadout is:

* 2 active abilities;
* 1 passive ability.

This target may be refined later through combat prototyping.

The game should avoid an excessive number of simultaneous abilities.

The design objective is to create meaningful decisions rather than mechanical overload.

---

## 7.4 Progressive ability unlocking

**LOCKED**

Weapon abilities are unlocked progressively.

Ability access depends on a combination of:

* weapon tier;
* weapon mastery.

The exact distribution of abilities between tier and mastery is not yet defined.

A higher-tier weapon may support a greater number of usable ability slots than an entry-level weapon.

Weapon mastery may unlock new ability choices or permit additional slots to be used.

The system must produce a visible feeling of growth when the player specializes in a weapon family.

---

## 7.5 Build diversity

**LOCKED AS DESIGN GOAL**

The game should avoid one universal build being optimal for every encounter.

Enemies and bosses should encourage different answers.

Examples include:

* more damage;
* more defense;
* faster attacks;
* sustain;
* resistance to a mechanic;
* a different weapon;
* a different active ability;
* a different passive;
* different food or potion choices.

The game should create build decisions without relying on dozens of opaque statistics.

---

# 8. WORLD STRUCTURE

## 8.1 Zone families

**LOCKED**

The world is divided into five major danger levels:

1. Blue Zone;
2. Yellow Zone;
3. Orange Zone;
4. Red Zone;
5. Black Zone.

Each color represents a major chapter of progression.

A zone is not merely a visual change or a different resource table.

Each zone must provide a complete progression arc.

---

## 8.2 Zone progression

**LOCKED**

Each zone contains a substantial number of stages.

The final number of stages will be defined later.

Stages may include different internal difficulty levels or variations if necessary, but the player-facing structure should remain clear.

The game will not use decimal progression labels such as:

* 1.1;
* 1.2;
* 1.3;
* 5.1;
* 5.2.

Preferred presentation:

* Blue Zone — Stage 1;
* Blue Zone — Stage 2;
* Blue Zone — Stage 3;
* Yellow Zone — Stage 1.

---

## 8.3 Zone chapters

**LOCKED**

Each color zone should function as a chapter containing a progression cycle such as:

* discovery;
* gathering;
* equipment creation;
* mastery progression;
* stage completion;
* optimization;
* mini-boss encounters;
* final power check;
* zone boss;
* access to the next zone.

The player should spend meaningful time progressing inside each zone.

---

## 8.4 Tier progression example

**LOCKED AS GENERAL DIRECTION**

The intended relationship between early zones and equipment tiers is similar to:

* Blue Zone: progression through approximately Tier 3 and Tier 4;
* end of Blue Zone: optimization of Tier 4 equipment;
* Yellow Zone: access to Tier 5 progression;
* later zones: access to increasingly powerful tiers.

The final tier distribution by zone will be defined later.

Obtaining the first item of a tier must not automatically mean the player is ready for the next zone.

The player may need to:

* complete a build;
* improve item quality;
* raise masteries;
* select better abilities;
* prepare consumables;
* defeat the zone boss.

---

## 8.5 Future zone content

The following will be defined in dedicated world and content documents:

* resources;
* gathering nodes;
* monster families;
* bosses;
* cities;
* settlements;
* dungeons;
* random events;
* special stages;
* elite enemies;
* environmental effects;
* loot tables.

No structural assumptions about these systems should be coded before validation.

# 9. PROGRESSION WALLS

## 9.1 Purpose

**LOCKED**

Progression walls are intentional tools used to create:

* anticipation;
* optimization;
* strategic learning;
* long-term goals;
* satisfaction when overcoming challenges.

Walls must feel understandable and surmountable.

They should not appear as arbitrary hidden restrictions.

---

## 9.2 Soft walls

**LOCKED**

A soft wall slows the player without completely stopping progression.

Examples:

* enemies take too long to defeat;
* resource collection is inefficient;
* repair costs reduce profitability;
* the player can survive but gains resources slowly;
* farming a stage produces poor results.

A soft wall signals that optimization would be beneficial.

---

## 9.3 Hard walls

**LOCKED**

A hard wall prevents meaningful progression.

Examples:

* a boss cannot be defeated;
* the character cannot survive the encounter;
* required damage cannot be reached with the current preparation;
* a mandatory stage cannot be completed.

A hard wall must have identifiable solutions.

It must not be bypassed only by waiting for offline rewards unless those rewards are part of a deliberate improvement plan.

---

## 9.4 Knowledge walls

**LOCKED**

A knowledge wall occurs when the player may possess enough total power but is using an unsuitable strategy.

Possible causes:

* wrong weapon;
* wrong ability;
* wrong passive;
* unsuitable armor;
* inappropriate consumable;
* poor defensive or offensive balance;
* failure to understand an enemy mechanic.

Knowledge walls should teach the player to adapt.

The game must provide enough information for the player to understand why they failed.

---

## 9.5 Power checks

**LOCKED**

Important stages, mini-bosses and bosses may act as power checks.

A power check evaluates one or more aspects of the player’s preparation.

Examples:

* damage check;
* survival check;
* sustain check;
* speed check;
* build check;
* consumable check;
* mastery check;
* strategy check.

Different power checks should encourage different improvements.

The answer must not always be additional raw damage.

---

# 10. DEATH AND FAILURE

## 10.1 Scaled consequences

**LOCKED**

The consequences of death increase according to zone danger.

General direction:

### Blue Zone

* no meaningful death penalty.

### Yellow Zone

* light durability penalty.

### Orange Zone

* stronger durability penalty.

### Red Zone

* severe durability penalty.

### Black Zone

* very severe durability penalty;
* significant risk of equipment becoming unusable or eventually breaking.

Exact values will be defined later.

---

## 10.2 Durability model

**LOCKED**

Death should primarily affect:

* current durability;
* maximum durability.

An item may gradually lose maximum durability after repeated failures in dangerous regions.

Example:

100 / 100
↓
Death
↓
80 / 99

This system replaces sudden, purely random item destruction as the primary death consequence.

The player should generally be able to see equipment degradation coming and react before permanent loss.

---

## 10.3 Permanent breakage

**CURRENT DIRECTION — DETAILS UNLOCKED**

Equipment may eventually become permanently unusable when its maximum durability reaches a defined threshold or zero.

The exact rules are not yet defined.

Claude must not implement permanent destruction values before the death and durability system is formally specified.

---

## 10.4 Risk and reward

**LOCKED**

Dangerous zones should provide better opportunities while exposing the player to stronger failure consequences.

The player must make an informed decision when selecting:

* farming location;
* equipment value;
* consumable investment;
* offline destination;
* acceptable death risk.

Risk should create tension without making ordinary progression feel unfair.

---

# 11. OFFLINE PROGRESSION

## 11.1 Base offline duration

**LOCKED**

The base offline progression cap is:

> 8 hours.

The final system must simulate or calculate progress accumulated during the player’s absence.

---

## 11.2 Expandable duration

**LOCKED**

The offline duration may be increased through permanent progression.

Possible sources include:

* island improvements;
* housing;
* beds;
* buildings;
* trophies;
* other validated upgrades.

Possible future thresholds may include:

* 10 hours;
* 12 hours;
* 16 hours;
* 24 hours.

These values are not yet final.

---

## 11.3 Offline design rule

**LOCKED**

Offline progression must reward preparation made before leaving.

The player should choose or configure the intended activity before going offline.

Examples may include:

* combat farming;
* gathering;
* refining;
* crafting;
* worker activity.

The exact list of offline-compatible activities will be defined later.

---

## 11.4 Offline risk

**UNRESOLVED**

The relationship between offline farming and death penalties requires a dedicated design decision.

Questions to resolve include:

* Can the character repeatedly die while offline?
* Does offline combat stop after the first death?
* Does the character retreat at a durability threshold?
* Can the player configure an automatic retreat rule?
* Can valuable equipment permanently break during an offline session?

Claude must not assume answers to these questions.

---

# 12. ECONOMY AND PRODUCTION

## 12.1 Purpose of the economy

**LOCKED**

The economy exists primarily to support character progression.

Resources are used for:

* equipment;
* tools;
* consumables;
* buildings;
* repairs;
* upgrades;
* future progression systems.

The game must not require a simulated player marketplace to function.

---

## 12.2 Gathering

**LOCKED AS CORE SYSTEM**

Gathering is a major progression activity.

It provides the raw materials required for crafting and advancement.

Gathering should include its own masteries and efficiency progression.

Exact gathering professions, resources and node mechanics will be defined later.

---

## 12.3 Refining

**LOCKED AS CORE SYSTEM**

Refining transforms raw resources into crafting materials.

The current direction is inspired by Albion’s cascading refinement structure, where higher-tier production may require lower-tier processed materials.

Exact formulas and resource chains will be defined later.

---

## 12.4 Crafting

**LOCKED AS CORE SYSTEM**

The player crafts much of the equipment and supplies required for progression.

Crafting must contribute directly to the hero’s development.

Crafting should not become an isolated production spreadsheet disconnected from adventure.

---

## 12.5 Workers

**LOCKED AS FUTURE CORE SYSTEM**

Workers support the player by performing limited production or resource-related activities.

Workers do not replace the hero.

The first worker implementation should remain simple.

The full worker system will be defined later.

---

## 12.6 Island

**LOCKED AS FUTURE CORE SYSTEM**

The island serves as a long-term support system for the hero.

Possible functions include:

* buildings;
* workers;
* offline duration upgrades;
* resource support;
* production improvements;
* storage;
* progression bonuses.

The island must not become a separate city-building game that overshadows the main adventure loop.

---

# 13. REWARD CADENCE

## 13.1 Core rule

**LOCKED**

Power progression may be slow, but rewards must be frequent.

The player must rarely feel that nothing meaningful is happening.

---

## 13.2 Progression beats

The final progression curve should contain different reward scales.

### Micro progression

Possible duration:

* seconds to a few minutes.

Possible rewards:

* loot;
* resource gains;
* small mastery progress;
* minor efficiency gains;
* combat completion.

### Medium progression

Possible duration:

* several minutes to a play session.

Possible rewards:

* recipe unlock;
* item upgrade;
* new enemy;
* new stage;
* ability unlock;
* mastery level.

### Macro progression

Possible duration:

* multiple sessions.

Possible rewards:

* major boss;
* new region;
* new tier access;
* major build upgrade;
* new production capability.

Exact timing must be determined through balancing and testing.

The example durations above are conceptual, not final requirements.

# 20. PROJECT SUMMARY

Albion Idle is a single-player idle RPG centered on one persistent hero.

The player explores increasingly dangerous regions, gathers resources, refines materials, crafts equipment, develops specialized masteries and prepares builds capable of overcoming progression walls and bosses.

The world is divided into five major danger zones:

* Blue;
* Yellow;
* Orange;
* Red;
* Black.

Each zone acts as a progression chapter containing multiple stages, resources, enemies, optimization requirements and major power checks.

The character has no traditional global level.

Progression comes from masteries and equipment.

Combat is semi-idle, with automatic basic behavior and limited strategic intervention.

Weapons define active and passive abilities.

The current target is two active abilities and one passive ability, unlocked progressively through weapon tiers and mastery.

Death consequences scale with zone danger and primarily affect current and maximum equipment durability.

Offline progression begins with an eight-hour cap and may be extended through long-term progression.

The game rewards patience, but it must reward preparation and optimization more strongly than passive waiting alone

The economy supports the hero.

The hero supports the adventure.

The adventure drives the entire game.
