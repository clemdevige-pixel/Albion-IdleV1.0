# 20. COMBAT SYSTEM

---

# Purpose

The Combat System is the primary gameplay system of the project.

Every other progression mechanic ultimately exists to improve combat performance.

The player gathers resources in order to craft equipment.

The player crafts equipment in order to become stronger.

The player becomes stronger in order to defeat stronger enemies.

Defeating stronger enemies unlocks new resources, new equipment, and new progression.

Combat is therefore the central pillar around which the entire game is designed.

Nothing should bypass this progression loop.

---

# Design Philosophy

The Combat System follows several fundamental design principles.

---

## Combat is Automatic

Combat is fully automated.

The player never manually controls:

- Movement
- Basic Attacks
- Target Selection
- Skill Rotation

Instead, the player prepares the character before entering combat.

Success comes from preparation rather than execution.

The gameplay focuses on strategic decisions instead of mechanical skill.

Examples of meaningful decisions include:

- Equipment
- Weapon Family
- Armor Combination
- Consumables
- Passive Skills
- Automatic Skill Configuration
- Destination
- Activity Selection
- Progression Pace

---

## Side-Scroller First

Combat is designed around a **2D side-scrolling environment**.

All gameplay occurs on a single horizontal axis.

Characteristics:

- Horizontal movement only
- Fixed vertical gameplay plane
- No free movement
- No jumping
- No dodging
- No vertical positioning
- No manual repositioning

This greatly improves readability while simplifying AI, collision detection and balancing.

---

## Combat Must Remain Readable

Readability has priority over visual spectacle.

Players may observe thousands of combat encounters during long idle sessions.

Visual overload creates fatigue.

Therefore:

Frequent events remain visually simple.

Rare events receive stronger presentation.

Examples:

Basic Attack

- Small animation
- Small damage numbers
- Minimal visual effects

Critical Hit

- Larger damage number
- Small impact effect

Boss Death

- Large animation
- Camera Shake
- Unique sound
- Enhanced loot presentation

Legendary Loot

- Dedicated notification
- Unique animation
- Distinct audio

The player should instantly recognize important events.

---

## Combat Rewards Preparation

The game never rewards fast reactions.

A stronger build naturally produces:

- Faster encounters
- Better survivability
- Higher farming efficiency
- Better resource generation
- Faster progression

Preparation always outperforms mechanical execution.

---

## Idle First

The game is fundamentally an Idle RPG.

Combat continues naturally without constant interaction.

Player interaction only exists through strategic decisions.

---

# Core Combat Loop

Every combat follows the same sequence.

```
Walking

↓

Encounter Detected

↓

Movement Stops

↓

Automatic Combat

↓

Enemies Defeated

↓

Loot Generated

↓

Loot Collected

↓

Movement Resumes
```

This loop repeats continuously throughout exploration.

---

# Exploration Integration

Outside combat, the Hero automatically walks toward the right side of the screen.

Movement is fully automatic.

The player never manually moves the Hero.

Walking pauses only when:

- Combat begins
- Gathering begins
- Exploration is manually paused
- Hero dies

Otherwise, exploration never stops.

---

# Open World Exploration

Open World zones are infinite.

The Hero continuously progresses through an endless sequence of Zone Segments.

Enemy encounters are procedurally generated.

The player decides when to stop farming.

Open World has no completion state.

---

# Instanced Activities

Instanced content has a beginning and an end.

Examples:

- Solo Dungeon
- Group Dungeon
- Hellgate
- Expedition
- Boss Arena
- Future Raid

Instanced activities may contain:

- Checkpoints
- Boss Rooms
- Completion Rewards
- Victory Screens

The Combat System remains identical regardless of activity type.

---

# Encounter Generation

Enemies are generated as predefined Encounter Groups.

The system never spawns random isolated enemies directly.

Instead, one Encounter is selected from the area's Encounter Table.

Example:

```
Forest T4

Encounter A

2 Wolves

Weight 25

----------------

Encounter B

4 Wolves

Weight 15

----------------

Encounter C

1 Dire Bear

Weight 8

----------------

Encounter D

3 Wolves

1 Alpha Wolf

Weight 5
```

Each generated Encounter represents one complete combat.

---

# Active Encounter Rule

Only one Encounter may be active simultaneously.

While an Encounter remains alive:

- No additional Encounter may begin.
- Exploration remains paused.
- New enemies cannot spawn.

Future versions may support multi-pulls.

Version 1 intentionally limits combat to one active Encounter.

Benefits:

- Simpler AI
- Better readability
- Easier balancing
- Deterministic offline simulation

---

# Encounter Detection

While exploring, the Hero constantly scans forward.

Enemy detection occurs only in the direction of progression.

Once an Encounter enters Detection Range:

- Walking immediately stops.
- Combat begins.
- Exploration pauses.

Enemies positioned behind the Hero cannot initiate combat.

---

# Hero Behaviour

The Hero is completely autonomous.

The Hero automatically:

- Detects enemies
- Selects targets
- Moves into attack range if necessary
- Performs Basic Attacks
- Uses Automatic Skills
- Switches targets
- Continues until every enemy dies

The player never directly controls combat.

---

# Combat Positioning

Combat occurs on a single horizontal axis.

Typical encounter:

```
Hero

-------------------->

Enemy Group
```

For melee combat:

```
Hero

⚔

Enemy
```

For ranged combat:

```
Hero

-------- Projectile -------->

Enemy
```

No vertical positioning exists.

---

# Target Selection

Version 1 uses a deterministic targeting system.

Priority:

Nearest Living Enemy

Once that enemy dies:

Select the next nearest living enemy.

Future weapon families may override this behavior.

The architecture supports custom targeting rules.

---

---

# Combat Initiation

Combat begins as soon as an enemy enters Attack Range.

Example:

Bow

Enemy located six units ahead.

↓

Bow immediately fires.

↓

Enemy advances toward the Hero.

↓

If still alive

↓

Melee engagement occurs.

Range therefore creates a meaningful tactical advantage.

---

# Horizontal Movement During Combat

Combat movement is minimal.

Characters only move horizontally.

Movement exists solely to reach valid attack range.

Once inside range:

Movement stops.

Characters attack until:

- Target dies
- Target moves
- New target is selected

There is no free repositioning.

---

# Movement Speed

Movement Speed controls exploration only.

Higher Move Speed provides:

- More encounters per hour
- More farming efficiency
- Faster progression

Move Speed never affects:

- Attack Speed
- Animation Speed
- Cooldowns

Combat uses separate statistics.

---

# Attack Speed

Attack Speed determines attack frequency.

Each unit attacks independently.

Hero and enemies maintain separate cooldown timers.

Combat is fully asynchronous.

No turn-based mechanics exist.

---

# Combat Distance

Distance calculations occur exclusively along the X axis.

Distance is used for:

- Detection
- Targeting
- Attack Range
- Skill Range
- Projectile Travel
- AI Decisions

No Y-axis calculations are required.

---

# Projectile System

Ranged attacks create gameplay projectiles.

Each projectile owns:

- Position
- Direction
- Speed
- Target
- Maximum Range
- Collision State

Projectiles only travel horizontally.

Damage is applied only upon collision.

The same system supports:

- Arrows
- Bolts
- Fireballs
- Frost Bolts
- Spears
- Future Boss Projectiles

One unified projectile architecture serves every ranged attack.

---

# Projectile Travel

Every projectile updates every simulation tick.

A projectile continues moving until:

- It collides with its target.
- Maximum range is reached.
- Lifetime expires.
- Another gameplay mechanic destroys it.

Projectiles never teleport unless explicitly defined by a Skill Definition.

Examples:

Arrow

Normal horizontal travel.

Fireball

Horizontal travel followed by explosion.

Holy Beam

Extremely high speed while still using the projectile pipeline.

---

# Combat States

Combat uses a deterministic finite state machine.

Version 1 supports:

Walking

↓

Combat

↓

Victory

↓

Walking

or

Walking

↓

Combat

↓

Defeat

Walking, Combat, Victory and Defeat are the only gameplay combat states.


---

# Automatic Skills

Combat skills belong to two categories.

## Automatic Skills

Automatic Skills are executed automatically.

The player never manually activates them.

Examples:

- Heal below 50% HP
- Defensive Shield at combat start
- Area Attack when three or more enemies are nearby
- Execute when target HP falls below a threshold

Every Automatic Skill defines:

- Activation Conditions
- Cooldown
- Priority
- Target Rules
- Range

Different weapon families therefore naturally exhibit different combat behaviors.

---

## Manual Skills

Some abilities may require player interaction.

Examples:

- Ultimate Abilities
- Emergency Shields
- Temporary Buffs
- Special Consumables

Manual Skills are optional.

Players may ignore them during normal farming.

More difficult encounters reward proper manual timing without compromising the Idle identity of the game.

---

# Skill Priority

Whenever a skill becomes available:

1. Verify cooldown.
2. Verify activation conditions.
3. Verify target availability.
4. Verify target range.
5. Execute the highest-priority valid skill.
6. Resume normal combat.

Only one action may be executed at any given moment.

Skills never interrupt one another.

---

# Basic Attacks

Whenever no valid skill can be executed:

The Hero performs a Basic Attack.

Every Weapon Family defines:

- Attack Animation
- Damage Formula
- Attack Speed
- Attack Range
- Projectile Behavior (if applicable)

Basic Attacks remain the primary source of damage during most encounters.

---

# Enemy Behaviour

Enemies are fully autonomous.

Every enemy owns:

- Statistics
- AI Profile
- Skills
- Attack Speed
- Attack Range
- Detection Range
- Movement Speed

Enemy behavior is completely data-driven.

Adding new monsters never requires Combat code modifications.

---

# Simultaneous Combat

Every enemy attacks independently.

Example:

Hero

vs

6 Wolves

Each Wolf maintains:

- Its own cooldown
- Its own target validation
- Its own attack timer

Enemies never wait for one another.

Large enemy groups therefore naturally increase combat difficulty.

This design increases the value of:

- Armor
- Healing
- Crowd Control
- Area Damage
- Sustain Builds

---

Important systems include:

- Sustain Builds

# Victory

Combat ends immediately after every enemy belonging to the current Encounter dies.

Victory automatically triggers:

- Loot Generation
- Fame Rewards
- Silver Rewards
- Experience Calculations
- Progression Updates

After reward distribution:

Walking resumes automatically.

# Inventory Interaction

Players decide whether to:

- Equip
- Sell

This preserves build diversity.

# Combat Pause

Combat cannot be interrupted.

While combat is active the player cannot:

- Change Equipment
- Modify Skills
- Leave Combat

After Victory:

Exploration may be paused before reaching the next Encounter.

---

Characteristics:

# Combat State

Combat begins when an Encounter activates.

Characteristics:

- Walking stops

- Skills become available
- Damage calculations occur
- Projectiles are simulated

Possible transitions:

Combat

↓

Victory

Walking

Combat

---

# Victory State

After the final enemy dies:

The Combat System processes:

- Loot
- Fame
- Silver
- Progression Rewards

When complete:

Walking resumes automatically.

---

# Defeat State

Combat immediately ends when Hero Health reaches zero.

Remaining enemies immediately stop attacking.

Death penalties depend entirely on World Rules.

Examples:

Blue Zone

Minimal penalty

Yellow Zone

Knockdown

Red Zone

World-defined penalty

Black Zone

World-defined penalty

Combat simply reports Hero Death.

Penalty processing belongs to World Systems.

---

# Paused State

Outside combat the player may pause progression.

While paused:

- Walking stops
- No new encounters generate
- Inventory remains accessible
- Equipment may be modified
- Skills may be changed

Resuming returns directly to Walking.

# Combat Readability

Visual clarity is considered a gameplay feature.

Players should always understand:

- Who attacked.
- Who received damage.
- Which skill was used.
- Why damage occurred.
- Why an enemy died.

Because the game is viewed from a fixed side-scrolling perspective, combat readability must remain excellent even during long idle sessions.

The following principles are mandatory:

- Characters must never overlap excessively.
- Attack animations must clearly indicate their direction.
- Damage numbers must never obscure the battlefield.
- Visual effects must never hide enemies.
- Important gameplay events must always take priority over cosmetic effects.

Gameplay information always has priority over visual spectacle.

---

---

# Camera Behaviour During Combat

The Combat System interacts with the Camera System without controlling it.

During combat the camera remains:

- Horizontally centered on the Hero.
- Vertically fixed.
- Smoothly interpolated.
- Bounded inside the current Zone Segment.

Camera shake is reserved for exceptional events.

Examples:

- Critical Hits
- Boss Spawn
- Boss Death
- Major Skills
- Legendary Drops

Normal attacks never trigger camera shake.

---

# Animation Behaviour

Combat animations prioritize responsiveness.

Every combat animation must:

- Start immediately.
- Clearly communicate the performed action.
- Finish quickly.
- Never delay gameplay simulation.

Version 1 uses intentionally simple animation sets.

Typical examples:

Hero

- Idle
- Walk
- Attack
- Cast
- Hurt
- Death

Enemy

- Idle
- Walk
- Attack
- Hurt
- Death

Characters simply flip horizontally according to facing direction.

Separate left/right animation sets are unnecessary.

---

# Collision Philosophy

Combat uses simplified collision.

Collision exists only for gameplay purposes.

Examples:

- Projectile collision
- Attack range validation
- Enemy contact
- Target acquisition

Visual sprites may overlap slightly without affecting gameplay.

Collision should remain deterministic and inexpensive to simulate.

---

# Performance Considerations

Combat must support extremely long play sessions.

Design goals include:

- Stable FPS
- Deterministic simulation
- Minimal garbage collection
- Predictable CPU usage
- Efficient collision checks
- Efficient projectile updates

Combat should comfortably simulate hundreds of thousands of encounters without requiring a restart.

Performance always has priority over unnecessary visual complexity.

---

# Deterministic Simulation

Combat simulation must always be deterministic.

Given:

- Identical character statistics
- Identical enemy statistics
- Identical encounter generation
- Identical random seed

The simulation must always produce identical results.

Deterministic simulation guarantees:

- Reliable offline progression
- Easier debugging
- Server validation
- Replay consistency
- Future multiplayer compatibility

# Summary

The Combat System is the central gameplay pillar of the project.

It has been designed around five fundamental principles:

- Automation
- Readability
- Determinism
- Preparation
- Side-Scroller Simplicity

The Hero continuously explores the world, automatically engages enemy encounters, defeats opponents through deterministic combat simulation, collects rewards, and resumes exploration without player micromanagement.

Every surrounding gameplay system—including Equipment, Crafting, Refining, Gathering, Workers, Player Island, Progression and Masteries—ultimately exists to strengthen this core gameplay loop.

The Combat System remains fully data-driven, modular and deterministic, allowing future gameplay systems to extend its capabilities without requiring architectural redesign.
