# 21. DAMAGE SYSTEM

---

# Purpose

The Damage System is responsible for processing every source of damage in the game.

It transforms validated Damage Requests into Health reduction while remaining completely independent from combat logic.

The Damage System never determines **why** damage occurs.

Instead, it receives requests generated by other gameplay systems.

Typical sources include:

- Basic Attacks
- Active Abilities
- Projectiles
- Enemy Abilities
- Boss Mechanics
- Future Environmental Hazards

Its purpose is to provide one deterministic damage pipeline shared by the entire game.

---

# Design Philosophy

The Damage System follows eight fundamental principles.

---

## Deterministic

Given identical:

- Simulation Tick
- Attacker Statistics
- Target Statistics
- Damage Type
- Damage Value

the Damage System always produces identical results.

Random damage is forbidden.

Critical hits are forbidden.

Damage variance is forbidden.

---

## Simple

Damage calculations should remain easy to understand.

Players should immediately understand:

- Why damage occurred
- Why damage was reduced
- Why an enemy survived or died

Simple systems are easier to balance and maintain.

---

## Modular

The Damage System only receives Damage Requests.

It never communicates directly with combat systems.

Examples:

Ability System

↓

Damage Request

↓

Damage System

or

Projectile System

↓

Damage Request

↓

Damage System

---

## Pipeline Driven

Every damage request follows exactly the same processing pipeline.

No gameplay mechanic may bypass it unless explicitly documented.

This guarantees deterministic combat.

---

## Data Driven

The Damage System contains no weapon-specific logic.

It only processes data contained inside Damage Requests.

Weapons, enemies and abilities never modify the pipeline.

---

## Predictable

Combat outcomes should always be estimable.

Increasing:

- Weapon Power
- Damage
- Armor
- Magic Resistance

must produce immediately visible results.

Luck never influences damage.

---

## Extensible

The architecture supports future mechanics without redesign.

Examples:

- Damage over Time
- Reflect Damage
- Shields
- Absorption
- Healing Reduction
- Damage Amplification

Version 1 intentionally excludes these mechanics.

---

## Presentation Independent

Damage simulation never depends on:

- FPS
- Animations
- Camera
- Visual Effects
- Audio

Gameplay remains identical regardless of presentation.

---

# Responsibilities

The Damage System is responsible for:

- Damage Validation
- Damage Processing
- Resistance Calculation
- Minimum Damage Enforcement
- Health Reduction
- Death Detection
- Damage Events

The Damage System is NOT responsible for:

- Ability Execution
- AI Decisions
- Combat Flow
- Healing
- Loot
- Respawn
- Status Effects
- Statistic Calculation

---

# Damage Sources

The Damage System never distinguishes gameplay origin.

Every source ultimately generates a Damage Request.

Typical examples:

- Basic Attack
- Active Ability
- Projectile Impact
- Boss Ability

Future mechanics follow the exact same rule.

---

# Damage Request

Every calculation begins with a Damage Request.

Typical fields:

- Attacker
- Target
- Damage Value
- Damage Type
- Source ID
- Simulation Tick

Optional metadata may also be included.

Damage Requests contain no gameplay logic.

---

# Damage Types

Version 1 supports three Damage Types.

- Physical Damage
- Magical Damage
- True Damage

Every request belongs to exactly one Damage Type.

---

# Physical Damage

Physical Damage is reduced by Armor.

Typical sources include:

- Sword
- Axe
- Spear
- Bow
- Crossbow
- Hammer
- Mace
- Dagger

No other resistance affects Physical Damage.

---

# Magical Damage

Magical Damage is reduced by Magic Resistance.

Typical sources include:

- Fire Staff
- Frost Staff
- Holy Staff
- Nature Staff
- Arcane Staff

Armor never affects Magical Damage.

---

# True Damage

True Damage ignores every resistance.

It directly reduces Health.

True Damage should remain extremely rare.

Typical future uses:

- Boss Mechanics
- Legendary Abilities
- Special Encounters

---

# Unsupported Damage Types

Version 1 intentionally excludes:

- Damage over Time
- Reflect Damage
- Execute Damage
- Environmental Damage
- Self Damage
- Healing Damage

These mechanics may be introduced later without redesigning the pipeline.

---

# Damage Pipeline

Every Damage Request follows the exact same sequence.

```
Damage Request

↓

Validation

↓

Determine Damage Type

↓

Apply Resistance

↓

Minimum Damage Rule

↓

Reduce Health

↓

Death Check

↓

Emit Events

↓

Finished
```

Every gameplay source uses this pipeline.

---

# Validation

Before calculation begins, every Damage Request is validated.

Required conditions:

- Attacker Exists
- Target Exists
- Target Alive
- Damage > 0
- Damage Type Valid

Invalid requests are immediately discarded.

No gameplay effects occur.

---

# Resistance Selection

Only one resistance may be applied.

Physical Damage

↓

Armor

Magical Damage

↓

Magic Resistance

True Damage

↓

No Resistance

The Damage Type completely determines the calculation.

---

# Armor

Armor protects only against Physical Damage.

It has no effect on:

- Magical Damage
- True Damage

Armor is expressed as a percentage.

---

# Magic Resistance

Magic Resistance protects only against Magical Damage.

It has no effect on:

- Physical Damage
- True Damage

Magic Resistance is expressed as a percentage.

---

# Resistance Cap

Armor cannot exceed:

80%

Magic Resistance cannot exceed:

80%

Higher values are automatically clamped.

This prevents complete immunity through equipment.

---

# Damage Formula

Version 1 uses a simple deterministic formula.

Physical Damage

```
Final Damage

=

Base Damage

×

(1 − Armor)
```

Magical Damage

```
Final Damage

=

Base Damage

×

(1 − Magic Resistance)
```

True Damage

```
Final Damage

=

Base Damage
```

No additional modifiers exist in Version 1.

---
# Damage Calculation

Once the Damage Type has been determined, the appropriate resistance is applied.

Only one resistance may ever participate in a calculation.

Version 1 contains no combined resistance calculations.

---

# Physical Damage Processing

Physical Damage always uses Armor.

Pipeline:

```
Incoming Damage

↓

Armor Reduction

↓

Final Damage
```

Example:

```
Incoming Damage

100

Armor

25%

↓

Final Damage

75
```

---

# Magical Damage Processing

Magical Damage always uses Magic Resistance.

Pipeline:

```
Incoming Damage

↓

Magic Resistance

↓

Final Damage
```

Example:

```
Incoming Damage

120

Magic Resistance

40%

↓

Final Damage

72
```

---

# True Damage Processing

True Damage ignores every resistance.

Pipeline:

```
Incoming Damage

↓

Ignore Resistances

↓

Final Damage
```

Example:

```
Incoming Damage

150 True Damage

↓

150 Final Damage
```

---

# Minimum Damage Rule

Every successful Damage Request inflicts at least:

1 Damage

Even if resistance calculations produce:

0 Damage

the final value becomes:

1 Damage

This guarantees that combat always progresses.

---

# Exceptions

The Minimum Damage Rule may only be bypassed by future mechanics.

Examples:

- Invulnerability
- Complete Immunity
- Damage Negation

Version 1 contains none of these mechanics.

---

# No Random Damage

Version 1 contains:

- No Damage Variance
- No Random Rolls
- No Random Multipliers

Example:

```
Attack One

100

↓

Attack Two

100

↓

Attack Three

100
```

Every identical attack produces identical damage.

---

# No Critical Hits

Critical Hits do not exist.

Version 1 contains:

- No Critical Chance
- No Critical Damage
- No Critical Multiplier
- No Critical Rating

Every attack is fully deterministic.

---

# No Accuracy System

Every validated Damage Request succeeds.

Version 1 contains:

- No Accuracy
- No Dodge
- No Miss Chance
- No Evasion

If the request reaches the Damage System, damage will always be applied.

---

# Health Reduction

After the final damage has been calculated:

Health is reduced.

Example:

```
Current Health

750

↓

Incoming Damage

120

↓

Remaining Health

630
```

Health can never become negative.

---

# Health Clamp

If incoming damage exceeds remaining Health:

```
Current Health

85

↓

Incoming Damage

200

↓

Remaining Health

0
```

Overflow damage is ignored.

Negative Health values are impossible.

---

# Death Check

Immediately after Health reduction:

The Damage System performs a Death Check.

```
Health > 0

↓

Alive
```

or

```
Health = 0

↓

Dead
```

No other condition determines death.

---

# Death Handling

The Damage System detects death.

It never resolves gameplay consequences.

Instead it emits a Death Event.

Other systems handle:

- Loot
- Progression
- Experience
- Respawn
- Achievements
- Quest Progress

This separation keeps the architecture modular.

---

# Sequential Processing

Each Damage Request is processed independently.

Example:

```
Player Attack

↓

Enemy Attack

↓

Projectile Impact

↓

Boss Ability
```

Every request completes its pipeline before the next begins.

Requests are never merged.

---

# Same Tick Processing

Multiple Damage Requests may occur during one Simulation Tick.

Processing order is determined by the Combat Simulation.

Each request:

- Validates
- Calculates Damage
- Reduces Health
- Checks Death

before the next request begins.

This guarantees deterministic combat.

---

# Damage Events

Every successful Damage Request emits events.

Version 1 supports:

- OnDamageReceived
- OnDamageApplied
- OnHealthReduced
- OnEntityKilled

Events never modify gameplay.

---

# Event Subscribers

Typical subscribers include:

- UI System
- Floating Damage Numbers
- Audio System
- VFX System
- Statistics
- Achievements
- Quest System
- Analytics

The Damage System never directly communicates with them.

---

# Runtime Validation

Every Damage Request is validated before execution.

Validation includes:

- Valid Attacker
- Valid Target
- Positive Damage
- Valid Damage Type

Invalid requests are safely discarded.

---

# Invalid Targets

Damage cannot be applied to:

- Dead Entities
- Removed Entities
- Missing References
- Invalid Runtime Objects

The request is ignored without affecting gameplay.

---

# Invalid Damage Types

Unknown Damage Types are rejected.

Development builds:

- Log Error

Release builds:

- Discard Request

Gameplay continues safely.

---

# Runtime Performance

The Damage System should avoid:

- Memory Allocations
- Dynamic Collections
- Reflection
- Runtime Type Checks

Recommended:

- Cached Runtime Data
- Fixed Damage Requests
- Constant-Time Processing

Every request should complete in predictable time.

---

# System Integration

The Damage System receives requests from:

- Ability System
- Projectile System
- Future Environmental Systems

It communicates outward only through events.

The Damage System never calls gameplay systems directly.

---
# Future Compatibility

The Damage System has been intentionally designed to support future mechanics without modifying its core architecture.

Future features simply extend the existing Damage Pipeline.

Examples include:

- Damage over Time
- Healing
- Reflect Damage
- Damage Absorption
- Shields
- Invulnerability
- Immunity
- Damage Amplification
- Damage Reduction Buffs
- Healing Reduction

Version 1 intentionally excludes these mechanics.

---

# Damage over Time

Future Damage over Time (DoT) effects will periodically generate standard Damage Requests.

Example:

```
Poison

↓

Periodic Damage Request

↓

Damage System

↓

Standard Damage Pipeline
```

The Damage System requires no special handling.

---

# Healing

Healing will use an independent Healing Pipeline.

Example:

```
Healing Request

↓

Healing System

↓

Increase Health
```

Healing is intentionally separated from Damage processing.

---

# Reflect Damage

Future Reflect mechanics will generate a new Damage Request targeting the original attacker.

Example:

```
Incoming Damage

↓

Reflect Effect

↓

New Damage Request

↓

Damage System
```

Reflection never bypasses the standard pipeline.

---

# Shields

Future Shields may intercept incoming damage before Health reduction.

Possible future pipeline:

```
Damage Request

↓

Shield Absorption

↓

Remaining Damage

↓

Health Reduction
```

Version 1 contains no Shields.

---

# Invulnerability

Future Invulnerability effects may completely cancel a Damage Request.

Pipeline:

```
Damage Request

↓

Invulnerability Check

↓

Request Cancelled
```

No Health reduction occurs.

---

# Immunity

Future Immunity effects may ignore specific Damage Types.

Examples:

- Physical Immunity
- Magical Immunity
- True Damage Immunity

These checks occur before Health reduction.

---

# Damage Amplification

Future Buffs and Debuffs may modify incoming damage before resistance calculations.

Example:

```
Incoming Damage

↓

Damage Modifier

↓

Resistance Calculation

↓

Health Reduction
```

Version 1 contains no damage modifiers.

---

Development builds should provide a Damage Inspector.

Typical information:

- Attacker
- Target
- Damage Type
- Base Damage
- Resistance Applied
- Final Damage
- Remaining Health
- Simulation Tick

This greatly simplifies balancing and debugging.

---

# Damage History

Development builds may optionally record recent Damage Requests.

Typical history fields:

- Timestamp
- Attacker
- Target
- Damage Type
- Final Damage
- Result

This history is intended exclusively for debugging.

---

# Replay Compatibility

Because the Damage System is deterministic:

Complete combat can be reconstructed from:

- Initial Game State
- Player Inputs
- Simulation Tick
- Damage Requests

Individual damage values never need to be stored.

They can always be recalculated.

---

# Save System Integration

The Damage System stores no persistent runtime data.

Only Health values are saved by the Save System.

Damage Requests are transient objects.

No unfinished Damage Request survives a save/load cycle.

---

# Acceptance Criteria

The Damage System is considered complete for Version 1 when:

- Every source of damage generates a Damage Request.
- Every request passes through the shared Damage Pipeline.
- Physical Damage uses Armor.
- Magical Damage uses Magic Resistance.
- True Damage ignores all resistances.
- Armor is capped at 80%.
- Magic Resistance is capped at 80%.
- Every successful attack deals at least 1 Damage.
- Health never becomes negative.
- Death is detected immediately after Health reduction.
- The Damage System emits deterministic gameplay events.
- Multiple Damage Requests are processed sequentially.
- Damage calculations never depend on rendering.
- Damage processing remains deterministic under identical simulation conditions.

---

# Summary

The Damage System provides a deterministic, modular and data-driven framework responsible for processing every source of damage in the game.

Its sole responsibility is to validate Damage Requests, apply the appropriate resistance calculations, enforce the minimum damage rule, reduce Health, detect death and emit gameplay events. By remaining independent from combat logic, abilities and presentation systems, it guarantees predictable combat behavior, simplified balancing and long-term scalability while remaining fully compatible with future mechanics such as Damage over Time, Shields, Healing, Reflect Damage and Invulnerability.
