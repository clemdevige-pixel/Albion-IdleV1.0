# 24. ENEMY SYSTEM

---

# Purpose

The Enemy System defines every hostile entity encountered throughout the game.

Enemies represent the primary obstacle to player progression.

Every combat encounter, regardless of activity, is built from Enemy Definitions.

The Enemy System is responsible for:

- Enemy Definitions
- Enemy Families
- Enemy Categories
- Enemy Statistics
- Enemy Scaling
- Enemy Abilities
- Enemy Rewards
- Enemy Visual Identity
- Enemy Audio Identity
- Enemy Generation Data

The Enemy System never executes gameplay logic.

Combat execution belongs to the Combat System.

Decision making belongs to the AI System.

Damage belongs to the Damage System.

Abilities belong to the Ability System.

---

# Design Philosophy

The Enemy System is built around seven core principles.

## Data Driven

Every enemy is entirely defined through data.

Creating a new monster should never require programming.

Designers should be able to add enemies by creating new data entries.

---

## Deterministic

Given identical inputs:

- Enemy
- Statistics
- Encounter
- Random Seed

The enemy always behaves identically.

This guarantees deterministic offline progression.

---

## Side-Scroller First

Enemies exist inside a 2D horizontal world.

Characteristics:

- Horizontal movement only
- Fixed vertical plane
- No jumping
- No vertical positioning
- No free navigation
- Simple collision

Every enemy is designed around this gameplay space.

---

## Family Identity

Every family possesses a unique gameplay identity.

Players should immediately recognize a family through:

- Appearance
- Animations
- Sound
- Combat Style
- Rewards

Enemy identity is more important than raw statistics.

---

## Modular Design

Enemies reuse existing gameplay systems.

Every enemy shares:

- Character System
- Combat System
- Ability System
- Damage System
- Status Effect System

No dedicated combat engine exists for enemies.

---

## Infinite Exploration

Enemies continuously appear during exploration.

Version 1 never relies on:

- Respawn Timers
- Spawn Points
- Persistent World Populations

Exploration continuously generates new encounters.

---

## Readability

Enemy readability has priority over realism.

Players should instantly understand:

- Enemy Family
- Enemy Category
- Current Action
- Current Target
- Dangerous Abilities

Visual clarity always comes first.

---

# Responsibilities

The Enemy System is responsible for:

- Enemy Templates
- Statistics
- Categories
- Families
- Reward Data
- Ability Lists
- Animation References
- Audio References
- Encounter Participation

The Enemy System is NOT responsible for:

- Combat Execution
- AI Decisions
- Damage Calculation
- Projectile Simulation
- Loot Generation
- Exploration Flow
- Zone Generation

---

# Enemy Definition

Every enemy is represented by a single Enemy Definition.

Each definition contains:

- Identifier
- Display Name
- Family
- Category
- Zone Tier
- Statistics
- Ability List
- Loot Table Reference
- Fame Reward
- Sprite Set
- Animation Set
- Audio Set

Every hostile entity follows this exact structure.

---

# Enemy Categories

Version 1 supports four categories.

- Normal
- Veteran
- Elite
- Boss

Categories represent combat difficulty rather than enemy type.

The same family may exist in every category.

Example:

Normal Wolf

Veteran Wolf

Elite Wolf

Boss Wolf

All belong to the Animal Family.

---

# Normal Enemies

Normal enemies represent the majority of encounters.

Characteristics:

- Standard Statistics
- Simple Ability Kit
- Frequent Generation
- Standard Rewards
- Small Health Pools

Examples:

- Wolf
- Skeleton
- Heretic Scout
- Ore Elemental

---

# Veteran Enemies

Veterans are stronger versions of Normal enemies.

Characteristics:

- Higher Health
- Higher Damage
- Improved Rewards
- Additional Ability

Veterans introduce moderate combat challenges.

---

# Elite Enemies

Elite enemies represent dangerous encounters.

Characteristics:

- High Statistics
- Multiple Abilities
- Improved Survivability
- High Fame
- Better Loot

Elite encounters should feel immediately recognizable.

---

# Boss Enemies

Bosses represent the strongest encounters in Version 1.

Characteristics:

- Highest Statistics
- Complete Ability Kit
- Large Health Pool
- Unique Presentation
- Best Rewards

Bosses occupy the fifth Encounter of every Zone Segment.

Each Zone Segment therefore ends with a Boss Encounter.

Future versions may introduce:

- Multiple Phases
- Arena Mechanics
- Summons
- Enrage Mechanics

Version 1 Bosses remain compatible with the standard combat pipeline.

---

# Enemy Families

Version 1 supports seven enemy families.

Resource Families

- Animals
- Elementals

Combat Families

- Heretics
- Keepers
- Morgana

- Demons

Each family possesses its own combat identity.

---

# Resource Families

After defeating them, players gather resources.

They replace traditional gathering nodes in dangerous areas.

# Family Identity

Each family defines:

- Visual Theme
- Animation Style
- Audio Style
- Ability Pool
- Loot Pool
- Combat Identity

Families remain completely independent from Categories.

This allows scalable enemy creation without multiplying systems.

---

# Zone Tier

Enemies never possess numerical levels.

Power depends exclusively on:

- Zone Tier
- Enemy Category

Example:

T3 Normal Wolf

T4 Veteran Skeleton

T5 Elite Keeper

T6 Ore Elemental

T7 Elite Morgana

T8 Boss Demon

No additional level system exists.

---
# Enemy Statistics

Every enemy owns the standard Character Statistics.

Examples include:

- Maximum Health
- Physical Damage
- Magical Damage
- Armor
- Magic Resistance
- Attack Speed
- Movement Speed
- Attack Range
- Critical Chance
- Critical Damage

Enemy statistics are stored directly inside the Enemy Definition.

Enemies never calculate their statistics from equipment.

Scaling modifiers are applied by the Progress Scaling System before combat begins.

---

# Enemy Equipment

Enemies do not own gameplay equipment.

Instead they contain:

- Base Statistics
- Ability Set
- Visual Equipment
- Loot Table

Visual Equipment exists only for presentation.

Examples:

A Heretic Knight may visually carry:

- Sword
- Shield
- Helmet

Internally these visuals never modify statistics.

Only the Enemy Definition determines gameplay values.

---

# Enemy Abilities

Version 1 enemies use the exact same Ability System as the player.

Every enemy may possess:

- One Basic Attack
- Up to two Active Abilities
- One Passive Ability

Abilities use:

- Shared Cooldown System
- Shared Target Validation
- Shared Damage Pipeline
- Shared Status Effect System
- Shared Projectile System
- Shared Animation Pipeline

Only the ability data differs.

# Enemy Positioning

Version 1 combat occurs exclusively on a horizontal axis.

Enemy positioning follows simple rules.

Enemies always spawn:

- In front of the Hero
- Inside the current Encounter
- Facing the Hero

Typical encounter:

```
Hero

------------------>

Wolf   Wolf   Alpha Wolf
```

Enemies never appear behind the player during a standard encounter.

---

# Enemy Facing Direction

Enemies always face their current target.

Because combat is horizontal:

Only two facing directions exist.

- Left
- Right

Sprite flipping replaces dedicated left/right animation sets.

---

# Enemy Movement

Enemies move automatically.

Movement only exists to reach valid attack range.

Enemy movement stops immediately when:

- Target enters attack range.
- Target dies.
- Enemy dies.
- Combat ends.

Enemies never wander during combat.

There is no free repositioning.

---

# Enemy Detection

Each enemy owns a Detection Range.

When the Hero enters Detection Range:

The enemy becomes active.

Typical sequence:

Idle

↓

Hero Detected

↓

Advance

↓

Reach Attack Range

↓

Attack

Detection occurs entirely on the horizontal axis.

---

# Enemy Lifecycle

Every enemy follows the same lifecycle.

```
Generated

↓

Spawned Inside Encounter

↓

Idle

↓

Hero Detected

↓

Combat

↓

Defeated

↓

Loot Generated

↓

Destroyed
```

Enemies never remain in the world after defeat.

---

# Continuous Generation

The World System continuously generates new encounters.

Enemy generation depends on:

- Zone Tier
- Biome
- Zone Segment
- Encounter Table
- Family Distribution

Version 1 does not use:

- Spawn Points
- Respawn Timers
- Persistent Populations

Every generated encounter is temporary.

---

# Zone Segment Integration

Exploration is divided into Zone Segments.

Each Zone Segment contains exactly five encounters.

Typical structure:

Encounter 1

↓

Encounter 2

↓

Encounter 3

↓

Encounter 4

↓

Boss Encounter

After the Boss dies:

The player may continue into the next Zone Segment.

Enemy generation immediately begins again.

---

# Encounter Membership

Enemies never exist independently.

Every generated enemy belongs to exactly one Encounter.

Examples:

Encounter A

- 3 Wolves

Encounter B

- 1 Bear

Encounter C

- 2 Skeletons
- 1 Necromancer

Combat always begins against one complete Encounter.

# Enemy Rewards

Every enemy owns:

- Loot Table
- Fame Reward

- Zone Tier
- Enemy Category

Fame progression always follows:

Normal

↓

Veteran

↓

Elite

↓

Boss

# Enemy Death

Enemy death occurs immediately when Health reaches zero.

Death sequence:

↓

Death animation

↓

Combat event emitted

↓

Loot generated

↓

Reward collection

↓

# Resource Enemies

Animals and Elementals possess an additional post-combat interaction.

Sequence:

Combat

↓

Enemy dies

↓

Gathering Node appears

↓

Player gathers automatically

↓

Resources collected

# Enemy Variants

Version 1 intentionally excludes random enemy variants.

Every identical enemy shares:

- Statistics
- Abilities
- Rewards
- Appearance
- Behavior

Example:

Every T6 Normal Wolf behaves identically.

This guarantees deterministic balancing.

---
# Future Compatibility

Future extensions may include:

- Enemy Affixes
- Champion Enemies
- Seasonal Variants
- World Bosses
- Raid Bosses
- Dynamic Events
- Environmental Modifiers
- Elite Patrols
- Rare Spawn Variants
- Summoned Creatures
- Companion Enemies
- PvP NPCs

These features extend existing Enemy Definitions rather than replacing them.

OnEnemyDamaged

OnEnemyAbilityUsed

OnEnemyKilled

---

# Error Handling

Invalid Enemy Definitions must never crash the game.

Examples:

- Missing Statistics
- Missing Ability
- Unknown Family
- Invalid Loot Table
- Invalid Animation Reference
- Missing Audio Reference

Development builds should report detailed validation errors.

Release builds should safely ignore invalid enemy definitions while logging diagnostics.

---

# Performance Considerations

Enemy simulation must support extremely long idle sessions.

Design goals include:

- Minimal CPU usage
- Stable memory consumption
- Deterministic simulation
- Data-oriented storage
- Fast encounter creation
- Efficient destruction
- Minimal runtime allocations

Enemy Definitions should be loaded once and reused throughout the entire session.

Generated enemies should contain only runtime state.

# Immutable Design Rules

The following principles define the Enemy System and must never change without redesigning the project.

Rule 01

Enemies are entirely data-driven.

Rule 02

Enemies never possess numerical levels.

Rule 03

Enemy power depends exclusively on Zone Tier and Enemy Category.

Rule 04

Version 1 supports four Enemy Categories.

Rule 05

Version 1 supports seven Enemy Families.

Rule 06

Animals and Elementals belong to the Resource Families.

Rule 07

Heretics, Keepers, Morgana, Undead and Demons belong to the Combat Families.

Rule 08

Enemies never own gameplay Equipment.

Rule 09

Visual Equipment has no gameplay impact.

Rule 10

Enemies use the exact same Ability System as the player.

Rule 11

Enemies exist only within Encounters.

Rule 12

Every Encounter belongs to a Zone Segment.

Rule 13

Each Zone Segment contains five Encounters.

Rule 14

The fifth Encounter is always a Boss.

Rule 15

Enemies never respawn.

Rule 16

The world continuously generates new Encounters.

Rule 17

Version 1 contains no Enemy Variants.

Rule 18

Combat occurs exclusively on a horizontal axis.

Rule 19

Enemy movement is automatic.

Rule 20

Enemy behavior remains deterministic under identical conditions.

# Summary

The Enemy System provides a fully data-driven framework for every hostile entity encountered throughout the game.

Enemies are organized through **Families**, **Categories** and **Zone Tiers**, allowing scalable progression without introducing traditional character levels.

Designed specifically for the game's side-scrolling architecture, every enemy operates on a single horizontal axis, uses the shared Character, Combat, Ability, Damage and Status Effect Systems, and participates in deterministic Encounter-based gameplay.

Version 1 intentionally focuses on simple, readable and highly reusable enemy definitions while leaving room for future expansion through Affixes, advanced Boss mechanics and entirely new enemy families.
