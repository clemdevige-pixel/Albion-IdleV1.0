# 27_PROGRESSION_SYSTEM

## Purpose

The Progression System defines how the player becomes stronger and unlocks harder content without a global character level.

Progression is produced by the interaction of combat, equipment, enchantments, masteries, production and the Player Island. The current runtime and validated balancing data are authoritative when numerical details evolve.

---

# Core Principles

- No global character level.
- Equipment is the primary source of immediate combat power.
- Masteries provide permanent specialization, bonuses and ability progression.
- Production exists to create the equipment and consumables required to overcome progression walls.
- World progression is divided into world bands, zones and segments.
- Difficulty walls intentionally create reasons to gather, refine, craft, enchant or use consumables.
- Progression and balance remain data-driven.

---

# Player Power

Combat power comes mainly from:

- weapon and its abilities;
- armor pieces;
- offhand when the weapon is one-handed;
- equipment tier;
- enchantment;
- mastery;
- consumables during assisted progression.

There is no Energy/Mana resource in the current combat model. Active abilities are governed by cooldowns.

An offhand is an independent equipment component. A one-handed weapon must not be permanently balanced as if one specific offhand were intrinsic to the weapon.

---

# Vertical and Horizontal Progression

Vertical progression increases combat capability:

- higher equipment tiers;
- enchantments;
- mastery bonuses;
- equipment optimization.

Horizontal progression expands options:

- new weapons;
- new offhands;
- armor choices;
- new crafting/refining/gathering options;
- island buildings and upgrades;
- workers and production choices.

---

# World Progression Structure

The progression hierarchy is:

World Band
→ Zone
→ Segment
→ Encounters

A zone is not merely a stat range. It owns a progression purpose and expected equipment envelope.

The current Blue world contains the validated progression baseline used to calibrate future bands.

---

# Validated Blue Progression Contract

| Step | Expected equipment | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Birch Forest | starter weapon alone | discovery; no meaningful friction |
| Early Dark Swamp | starter weapon | first progression wall |
| Swamp S2-S6 | 1-2 T3 pieces | each craft buys additional segments |
| Swamp S6-S10 | full / near-full T3 | T3 consolidation |
| Swamp S10 | full T3 | clear possible without potion |
| After Swamp | Island N2 / T4 production | production pivot |
| Stone Highlands | T3 → first T4 pieces | transition; potion may bridge the initial wall |
| Golden Steppe | full T4.0 → first enchantments | T4 consolidation |
| Frostpeak | T4.1 → T4.2 | final Blue difficulty ramp |
| Frostpeak S10 | T4.2 assisted/optimized; T4.3 reliable AFK | end of Blue |

This table is a design contract, not a substitute for runtime benchmarking.

---

# Progression Walls

A wall is intentional when it communicates the next useful player action.

Valid responses to a wall may include:

- craft another armor piece;
- improve equipment tier;
- enchant equipment;
- increase relevant mastery;
- use a health potion for an assisted push;
- change weapon/loadout.

A wall is unhealthy when the required solution is unclear, when the previous upgrade provides no visible progress, or when a single weapon/loadout bypasses the intended progression by an excessive margin.

Consumables are allowed to bridge a difficult transition. They must not automatically redefine the expected AFK equipment threshold.

---

# Island and Building Progression

Island progression is linked to world/zone progression.

When the required world progression cap is reached, the Island may level up. Building tier progression is then gated by the Island level.

Therefore the macro loop is:

Combat progression
→ Island level eligibility
→ Building tier eligibility
→ Production upgrade
→ Better equipment
→ Further combat progression

This keeps the Island subordinate to and supportive of the hero progression loop.

---

# Mastery

There is no universal character XP level.

Masteries are activity/equipment-specific permanent progression. They may provide ability unlocks, bonuses and specialization according to their own authored data.

Do not duplicate mastery tier thresholds in this document. The runtime mastery/content data is authoritative.

Balance benchmarks must use mastery levels representative of the tested progression checkpoint rather than assuming a fixed universal mastery value.

---

# Equipment Tier and Enchantment

Tier and enchantment are distinct progression axes.

Tier represents the equipment generation/content tier.

Enchantment increases the power of eligible equipment within that tier and is an important vertical progression tool, especially late in a world band.

The current Blue calibration deliberately transitions from T4.0 to T4.1/T4.2/T4.3 rather than introducing a new tier inside Blue.

Future world bands should author their own expected tier/enchantment progression contract before numerical tuning begins.

---

# Weapon Diversity

Different weapons are allowed to have different combat identities.

For comparable weapons, a performance delta of roughly 5-10% can be acceptable when justified by identity, burst profile, utility, handling or defensive trade-offs.

Do not balance solely from sustained DPS.

Evaluation should consider:

- sustained DPS;
- opener/burst windows;
- ability contribution;
- handling (1H/2H);
- defensive package;
- offhand contribution;
- actual runtime clear behavior.

Weapon-only power and weapon+offhand loadout power must remain separately measurable.

---

# Runtime Validation

Synthetic arithmetic is useful for diagnosis, but progression acceptance is determined with the exact live CombatRuntime.

Reusable tools:

- `apps/client/src/runtime/CombatRuntimeBenchmarkHarness.ts`
- `apps/client/src/data/weaponIdealBenchmark.ts`
- `apps/client/src/data/weaponPackageBenchmark.ts`

Zone-specific sweeps are scenarios built on the generic runtime harness. Future bands must reuse the harness instead of creating band-specific simulation engines.

---

# Data-Driven Requirements

Numerical progression values belong in authored data/configuration, not UI or scene conditionals.

Current combat progression curves are authored in:

`packages/data/src/config/combat-progression.ts`

Content identity and zone definitions remain in their dedicated data catalogs.

Adding a future zone or weapon should normally require data + benchmark scenarios, not new combat rules.

---

# Acceptance Rules for Future Progression

Before a new world band is considered balanced:

1. define its intended equipment/enchantment progression contract;
2. benchmark representative weapons/loadouts with the live runtime;
3. verify the entry wall and each meaningful upgrade checkpoint;
4. distinguish assisted clears (potions/manual intervention) from reliable AFK clears;
5. verify weapon deltas and outliers with weapon/package scoring;
6. adjust authored data, not the benchmark, to reach the intended contract;
7. retain regression tests for validated checkpoints.

---

# Summary

Albion Idle progression is a sequence of readable walls and power responses rather than a global level treadmill.

Combat exposes a need. Production, equipment, enchantment, mastery, consumables and loadout choices answer that need. The Island unlocks production capacity according to world progression, and improved production feeds the hero back into combat.

The validated Blue curve is the first reference implementation of this philosophy and the calibration template for future world bands.
