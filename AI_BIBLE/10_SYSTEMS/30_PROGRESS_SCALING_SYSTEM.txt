# 30. PROGRESS SCALING SYSTEM

---

# Purpose

The Progress Scaling System defines how combat difficulty rises across regions/zones and how equipment progression is expected to answer that difficulty.

The previous infinite-Cycle model is not the current implementation and must not be used as a balancing reference.

Current progression is authored through world bands, zones, segments, equipment tiers, enchantments and mastery.

---

# 1. Current progression layers

PLAYER POWER
- Weapon/equipment base tier.
- Equipment stat distribution.
- 1H + explicit off-hand combinations.
- Enchantment.
- Weapon/family mastery.
- Ability unlocks/mechanics.
- Consumables when the progression contract expects them.

WORLD DIFFICULTY
- World band.
- Zone placement inside the band.
- Segment position.
- Encounter position/content.
- Authored health/damage/defense curve.
- Optional authored final-boss gate pressure.
- Enemy family/category/content modifiers already used by the live pipeline.

These layers remain independent and data-driven.

---

# 2. World-band architecture

Combat progression curves are authored in:
- packages/data/src/config/combat-progression.ts

WORLD_COMBAT_PROGRESSION maps an authored world band to:
- its zone curve set;
- reward-rank offset.

A world band without authored combat progression must fail explicitly. It must never silently reuse Blue balance.

When later bands are balanced, author their own curve data and validate them with the same generic runtime benchmark.

---

# 3. Zone curve model

Each zone owns:
- healthStart / healthEnd;
- damageStart / damageEnd;
- defenseStart / defenseEnd;
- optional bossGate for the final zone boss.

Difficulty interpolates through the existing progression/runtime pipeline across the zone's segments/encounters.

The curve defines shape, not design meaning. The design meaning is established by progression checkpoints such as:
- starter clear;
- first wall;
- first armor craft value;
- full-tier clear;
- potion-assisted bridge;
- enchantment wall;
- AFK/farm threshold;
- final Tier boss gate.

A bossGate is data-driven and applies only to the final boss encounter. It may increase HP, damage and defense independently without changing the normal segment curve.

---

# 4. Blue world — validated progression shape

BIRCH FOREST
- Starter weapon only.
- Discovery zone.
- S10 clearable without potion across representative starters.

DARK SWAMP
- Early zone creates first starter wall.
- One/two T3 crafts buy meaningful progress.
- Quasi/full T3 consolidates late Swamp.
- Full T3 clears S10 without potion.

STONE HIGHLANDS
- Transition T3 -> T4.
- Full T3 can use potion as an entry bridge.
- First T4 pieces progressively remove that dependency.
- Full T4.0 clears the zone.

GOLDEN STEPPE
- Full T4.0 is the early/mid baseline.
- First enchantments become the intended late-zone progression tool.
- T4.1 is the late Steppe target.

FROSTPEAK / MOUNTAIN
- T4.1 enters/progresses.
- T4.2 reaches late Frostpeak but must not defeat the final Tier boss, even with potion.
- The final boss is an explicit T4.3 gear gate.
- Full T4.3 + potion must clear across the representative weapon roster.
- T4.3 without potion is not guaranteed and may vary by weapon profile.
- The validated Frostpeak bossGate is HP x1.575, damage x1.40, defense x1.10.

This sequence is the current Blue reference model for designing later bands: upgrades must create visible progression, and the final Tier boss certifies completion of the current equipment Tier.

---

# 5. Wall design

A progression wall is intentional only if the player has a clear next action.

Valid next actions include:
- craft one or more relevant armor pieces;
- upgrade weapon/equipment tier;
- enchant equipment;
- improve mastery;
- use a potion when the checkpoint contract allows it;
- optimize a legitimate build/off-hand choice.

A wall is unhealthy when:
- every available upgrade gives negligible progress;
- one mandatory item is hidden from the player;
- the wall exists because a benchmark or runtime is using incorrect player stats;
- the global zone is changed to compensate for one weapon outlier.

Normal progression remains monotonic by default.

Final zone bosses are an explicit exception: a bossGate may create a local difficulty spike above the normal segment envelope. The following zone is compared to the preceding normal progression, not to the exceptional boss spike. Therefore a next-zone entry may legitimately be easier than the previous zone's final boss.

The boss gate must remain a combat check, not an artificial inventory rule. The game must not require a hardcoded count of Tn.3 items; the authored combat pressure should make optimized Tn.3 the natural requirement.

---

# 6. Potion bridge, AFK threshold and boss gate

POTION BRIDGE
A build below an autonomous baseline may use live potions to cross a normal transition temporarily when the authored checkpoint allows it.

AFK THRESHOLD
The intended farm profile clears normal farm content without potion dependency.

BOSS GATE
A final Tier boss validates completion of the current Tier. For the current Frostpeak contract:
- T4.2 + potion must fail across the representative roster.
- T4.3 + potion must clear across the representative roster.
- T4.3 without potion may pass or fail depending on weapon identity.

Boss-gate potion semantics are separate from normal AFK farming semantics. A player may farm the late zone autonomously while still needing a potion for the one-time/progression boss check.

---

# 7. Weapon variance and zone scaling

Zone curves must be calibrated against a representative set of weapons, not a single weapon.

Same-class weapon variance around roughly 5-10% is acceptable when it represents identity.

If one weapon fails while the rest meet a checkpoint:
1. inspect weapon-only sustained/opener diagnostics;
2. inspect neutral-package score;
3. inspect explicit loadout/off-hand score;
4. inspect live TTK/HP result;
5. only change the zone if the evidence shows the zone contract itself is wrong.

Do not flatten weapon identities to make every checkpoint produce identical HP percentages.

Boss gates are validated against the full representative roster. The contract protects access for every representative weapon at the intended optimized loadout, not identical remaining HP.

---

# 8. Future band workflow

For each future band:
1. Define the expected tier/enchantment journey before numbers.
2. Define entry, mid, late and final-boss checkpoints per zone.
3. State potion semantics for each wall.
4. Decide whether the final boss is a Tier boss gate.
5. Author the band's normal combat curve and optional bossGate in combat-progression.ts.
6. Use CombatRuntimeBenchmarkHarness against the representative weapon set.
7. Use weaponPackageBenchmark diagnostics for outliers.
8. Iterate data only.
9. Validate the entire previous band to prevent regression at the boundary.
10. Freeze the semantic contract in tests/documentation.

Do not create a region-specific combat simulation or duplicate formulas.

---

# 9. Tier / enchantment philosophy

Higher tiers remain meaningful vertical progression where the content calls for them.
Enchantments provide intermediate vertical steps inside a tier.
Mastery remains an independent progression axis.

The relative importance of tier, enchantment and mastery can differ by world band, but each must remain visible and testable.

For Tier-transition bosses, optimized Tn.3 is the expected completion state before the player reliably moves into Tn+1 production/progression.

A later band may require T5/T6/etc.; this does not alter the architecture. Add the authored content and checkpoints, then reuse the same tools.

---

# Summary

Current Albion Idle scaling is world/zone/segment based, with gear tiers, enchantments, mastery and consumables forming the player response. Normal progression remains monotonic, while authored final-boss gates may deliberately create local difficulty spikes that certify completion of the current Tier. These gates are data-driven and validated through the exact live runtime across the representative weapon roster.
