# 33. ENCHANTMENT SYSTEM

## Purpose

The Enchantment System upgrades an existing equipment instance beyond its base Tier while preserving Tier identity.

Enchantments are permanent, item-specific, guaranteed, sequential, resource-intensive and data-driven.

V1 uses enchantment levels `.0` to `.3`. Level `.4` remains structurally reserved for future endgame design and is not currently obtainable.

---

# Validated V1 Rules

| Level | Display | Cumulative Item Power bonus | Incremental Tier Shard cost |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 0 | .0 | +0 IP | - |
| 1 | .1 | +50 IP | 10 |
| 2 | .2 | +100 IP | 30 |
| 3 | .3 | +150 IP | 70 |
| 4 | .4 | Reserved | Reserved |

A `.0 -> .3` progression consumes 110 enchantment shards total.

The validated V1 baseline is +50 Item Power per active enchantment level. With the current conversion this corresponds approximately to +10% / +20% / +30% cumulative numerical power at `.1` / `.2` / `.3`.

Enchanting strongly optimizes equipment inside its Tier without replacing Tier progression. In the current balance, a T5.0 item remains stronger than the corresponding T4.3 item.

`.3` must not be required to unlock the next Tier. It is an optimization goal, not a progression gate.

---

# Tier Eligibility

V1 enchantment starts at Tier 4 and supports T4 through T8 resources.

Equipment definitions remain authoritative for whether a specific item/category is enchantable and for its maximum enchantment level. The architecture supports weapons, off-hands, helmets, chest armor, boots and capes when enabled by their data.

---

# Sequential Progression

Valid V1 transitions are `.0 -> .1`, `.1 -> .2` and `.2 -> .3`. `.3 -> .4` is reserved for future design. Levels cannot be skipped.

Enchanting does not change the item's base Tier, quality, identity or equipment category.

---

# Guaranteed Success

Every valid enchantment succeeds. There is no failure chance, critical failure, downgrade, item destruction, fail stack, pity system, random material loss or regression.

Difficulty comes from obtaining the required resources, materials and Silver, not from gambling the item.

---

# Tier-Specific Enchantment Resource

V1 uses exactly one dedicated combat-earned enchantment resource per Tier:

- Enchantment Shard T4
- Enchantment Shard T5
- Enchantment Shard T6
- Enchantment Shard T7
- Enchantment Shard T8

A shard can only pay for equipment of its own Tier. There is no transmutation or conversion between shard Tiers.

The former universal Essence / Crystal / Catalyst model is obsolete and must not be used by active gameplay. Legacy copies may only exist transiently for save migration and are removed when old saves are loaded/imported.

---

# Enchantment Costs

Every active upgrade consumes matching Tier shards, refined craft materials associated with the equipment, and Silver.

The shard is the primary combat-time limiter. Refined materials preserve interaction with Gathering / Refining / Crafting. Silver provides an economic sink. These three costs should not all be tuned as extreme bottlenecks simultaneously.

Validated incremental shard curve:

- `.0 -> .1`: 10 shards
- `.1 -> .2`: 30 shards
- `.2 -> .3`: 70 shards

Shard quantities do not scale by weapon category or equipment Tier. Tier/category differences may instead affect Silver and refined-material costs.

Current refined-material quantity multipliers are x1 / x2 / x4 for `.1` / `.2` / `.3`. Silver increases with enchantment level and may additionally scale with Tier and equipment category through data.

---

# Resource Acquisition

Enchantment shards are combat loot from content corresponding to their Tier:

- T4 content / Blue progression -> T4 shards
- T5 content / Yellow progression -> T5 shards
- T6 content -> T6 shards
- T7 content -> T7 shards
- T8 content -> T8 shards

Normal monsters may drop shards. Elite enemies and bosses should provide better expected enchantment-resource yield than ordinary enemies.

Enchantment shards are NOT awarded simply for completing a segment.

---

# Anti-Trivial-Farm Rule

A universal fixed drop chance per monster is forbidden because it would make the weakest / fastest-killed monster the optimal enchantment farm.

Drop balancing must be evaluated through expected enchantment shards per unit of time using actual monster difficulty / TTK and spawn cadence.

Progressing deeper into content of the same Tier must be at least competitive with farming its earliest trivial segment and should generally provide a modestly better expected yield when difficulty increases.

The current implementation uses provisional difficulty weighting based on monster profile/depth with elite and boss modifiers. Final rates are deliberately NOT locked until the dedicated monster/TTK balance pass is performed with runtime data.

---

# Item Power and Stat Interaction

Each active enchantment level adds +50 Item Power:

- `.0`: +0 IP
- `.1`: +50 IP
- `.2`: +100 IP
- `.3`: +150 IP

The Stat System remains authoritative for converting Item Power into final numerical statistics.

Enchantments do not automatically unlock abilities, change ability identity/slots, grant Mastery XP, change Tier or change Quality. Mastery, Tier, Quality and Enchantment remain independent progression dimensions.

---

# Item-Specific State

Enchantments belong to the exact equipment instance. They cannot be transferred, extracted, copied, duplicated, moved to another item or inherited by a replacement item.

A superior replacement item must be enchanted independently.

Enchantable equipment uses persistent instance identity; item type, Tier, quality or inventory position alone are not authoritative identities.

---

# Upgrade Validation

Before an upgrade commits, V1 validates at minimum:

- Item instance exists.
- Item is enchantable.
- Next level is valid and enabled.
- Item has not reached its configured cap.
- Player owns enough matching Tier shards.
- Player owns required refined materials.
- Player owns enough Silver.
- Required inventory capacity exists when necessary.
- Enchantment is currently permitted by combat state.
- Transaction has not already been processed.

Future systems may add reservation, inventory-lock, server/save-transaction or other restrictions without changing the core enchantment rules.

---

# Combat Restriction

Enchanting is forbidden for the entire duration of an active combat segment, including transitions between monsters.

The rule uses authoritative combat stop/segment state rather than a transient presentation state such as whether an enemy is visible on the current frame.

- Segment `running` -> forbidden.
- `stop_requested` -> forbidden.
- Segment fully `paused` / stopped -> allowed, subject to other validations.

This prevents enchanting during the transition window between two monsters.

---

# Equipped Items

The current architecture may enchant an equipped item when it can safely resolve/update its persistent instance and the player is outside an active combat segment.

Unequipping is not an intrinsic V1 design requirement.

---

# Transaction Safety

Enchanting is intended to behave as one deterministic transaction: upgrade exactly one item by one level, consume exactly the required materials and Silver, and never process the same transaction identifier twice.

Transaction identifiers protect against duplicate requests/double clicks.

The implementation must continue moving toward strict atomic commit/rollback guarantees. A failure during the commit sequence must never become an accepted gameplay state where only part of the transaction has persisted.

---

# Upgrade Interface

The interface should expose selected equipment, current `Tn.x`, next `Tn.x`, current/projected stats, required/owned/missing materials, Silver cost, blocking reason and confirmation action.

The result must be predictable before confirmation. Technical failure keys must not be exposed directly to the player; UI-facing reasons use readable/localized messages.

---

# Visual Language

Tier and Enchantment use two independent visual channels.

## Equipment Halo / Frame = Base Tier

- T3: green
- T4: blue
- T5: red
- T6: orange
- T7: yellow
- T8: white

Enchanting an item must never change its Tier halo/frame.

## `Tn.x` Text Color = Enchantment Level

- `.0`: white / grey
- `.1`: green
- `.2`: blue
- `.3`: purple
- `.4`: yellow

Example: a T4.3 item keeps the T4 blue halo while its `T4.3` label uses the `.3` purple enchantment color.

These presentation rules are shared across equipment UI surfaces.

---

# Persistence and Legacy Migration

Enchantment level is persistent item-instance state. Current saves persist enchantment level rather than baked Item Power, so changing the global IP baseline does not require rewriting each enchanted item instance.

Legacy universal resources (Essence / Crystal / Catalyst) are removed from old player storage rather than converted into new Tier shards. There is intentionally no legacy-resource-to-shard conversion.

---

# Repair / Salvage / Reversal

Enchanting must not act as a free repair mechanic. Unless a future rule changes this, durability semantics are preserved.

If Salvaging later returns enchantment resources, recovered value must remain below total invested value and must not create a closed generation loop.

V1 does not support lowering an item's enchantment level.

---

# Future `.4`

`.4` remains structurally reserved for future endgame design. Current V1 rules intentionally do not define its resource cost, special components, Item Power bonus, unlock condition or acquisition loop.

Do not infer `.4` by automatically extending the `.1/.2/.3` curve. When `.4` is designed, this document and authoritative data must be updated together.

---

# Current Closure Status

Validated during the Enchanter implementation pass:

- Deterministic guaranteed upgrades.
- Sequential `.0 -> .1 -> .2 -> .3` progression.
- +50 IP per active enchantment level.
- One Tier-specific enchantment shard for each Tier T4-T8.
- No shard transmutation between Tiers.
- 10 / 30 / 70 incremental shard costs.
- Silver and refined materials retained as secondary sinks.
- Combat-segment restriction uses authoritative segment state.
- Old universal resources removed from active gameplay and handled only by legacy-save cleanup.
- Tier halo and enchantment-label colors separated.
- Automated typecheck/tests and manual gameplay validation completed during implementation.

Intentionally deferred:

- Final per-monster shard drop rates, pending dedicated monster/TTK balance.
- `.4` endgame design.
- Future market/salvage integrations.
- Stronger transactional rollback/atomicity hardening if required by persistence/server evolution.
