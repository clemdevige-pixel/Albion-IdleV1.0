# 33. ENCHANTMENT SYSTEM

## Purpose

The Enchantment System defines how equipment is progressively upgraded beyond its base Tier.

Enchantments represent the primary vertical progression system of the late game and very late game.

The system allows players to improve existing equipment without introducing Tier 9, Tier 10, or infinite equipment inflation.

Enchantments are:

- Permanent
- Item-specific
- Guaranteed
- Sequential
- Resource-intensive
- Fully data-driven

---

# Design Goals

The Enchantment System must:

- Provide long-term vertical progression.
- Avoid random upgrade failures.
- Preserve the value of crafting.
- Create permanent demand for multiple resources.
- Support a strong Silver sink.
- Keep progression readable.
- Prevent enchantment transfer exploits.
- Avoid endless enchantment inflation.
- Support future equipment categories.

---

# Core Philosophy

Enchantments are a long-term investment into a specific item.

The player does not gamble for power.

Instead, the player gathers the required resources, pays the required costs, and receives a guaranteed upgrade.

The challenge comes from:

- Resource acquisition
- Silver investment
- Material rarity
- Progression time
- Economic planning

It does not come from random failure.

---

# Enchantment Levels

The game supports five enchantment states.

| Enchantment Level | Display Format |
|---:|---|
| 0 | .0 |
| 1 | .1 |
| 2 | .2 |
| 3 | .3 |
| 4 | .4 |

The maximum enchantment level is `.4`.

Examples:

- T4.0
- T5.1
- T6.2
- T7.3
- T8.4

No enchantment level above `.4` is planned.

---

# Maximum Equipment State

The highest planned equipment state is:

T8.4

An item may additionally possess any valid Quality.

Examples:

- T8.4 Normal
- T8.4 Good
- T8.4 Outstanding
- T8.4 Excellent
- T8.4 Masterpiece

Quality and Enchantment remain independent.

---

# Equipment Creation Rule

All crafted equipment is initially created at enchantment level `.0`.

Examples:

- T4.0 Sword
- T6.0 Plate Armor
- T8.0 Staff

Crafting does not directly generate enchanted equipment.

The Enchantment System is a separate post-crafting process.

---

# Item Lifecycle

The intended equipment lifecycle is:

Gather Resources

↓

Refine Resources

↓

Craft Base Equipment

↓

Generate Permanent Quality

↓

Gather Enchantment Materials

↓

Upgrade to .1

↓

Upgrade to .2

↓

Upgrade to .3

↓

Upgrade to .4

This separates crafting excellence from late-game vertical progression.

---

# Sequential Progression

Enchantments follow a strictly linear progression.

An item may only be upgraded to the next enchantment level.

Valid transitions:

- .0 → .1
- .1 → .2
- .2 → .3
- .3 → .4

Invalid transitions:

- .0 → .2
- .0 → .4
- .1 → .3
- .2 → .4

No enchantment level may be skipped.

---

# Upgrade Validation

Before an upgrade begins, the system must verify:

- The item is enchantable.
- The item is below enchantment level .4.
- The requested level is exactly CurrentLevel + 1.
- The player owns every required material.
- The player owns enough Silver.
- The item is not currently locked by another operation.
- The item instance still exists.
- The player has sufficient inventory capacity for any returned outputs.
- Any additional system-specific requirement is satisfied.

If any validation fails, no resources are consumed.

---

# Guaranteed Success

Every valid enchantment upgrade succeeds.

There is no:

- Failure chance
- Critical failure
- Downgrade
- Item destruction
- Material loss on failure
- Enchantment instability
- Random regression

Once all requirements are met and the transaction is confirmed, the item advances exactly one enchantment level.

---

# No Random Success System

The following mechanics are explicitly excluded:

- Percentage-based upgrade success
- Fail stacks
- Pity systems
- Protection scrolls
- Item destruction prevention
- Downgrade protection
- Random enhancement stones

The Enchantment System is deterministic.

---

# Item-Specific Enchantments

Enchantments are permanently attached to the exact item instance that receives them.

They cannot be:

- Transferred
- Extracted
- Copied
- Duplicated
- Moved
- Swapped
- Inherited
- Recovered from destruction
- Applied to another item

---

# Transfer Example

The player owns:

T8.4 Good Sword

The player later obtains:

T8.0 Masterpiece Sword

The player cannot transfer the `.4` enchantment from the Good Sword to the Masterpiece Sword.

The Masterpiece Sword must be upgraded independently.

---

# Economic Consequence

Each optimized item requires its own complete enchantment investment.

This creates persistent demand for:

- Base equipment
- High-quality crafts
- Enchantment materials
- Silver
- Gathering
- Refining
- Crafting services

A new superior-quality base item does not invalidate the economic journey required to enchant it.

---

# Quality Independence

Quality never changes during enchantment.

Examples:

T8.0 Good

↓

T8.1 Good

↓

T8.2 Good

↓

T8.3 Good

↓

T8.4 Good

The item remains Good throughout the entire process.

Likewise:

T8.0 Masterpiece

↓

T8.4 Masterpiece

The Quality System and Enchantment System never modify each other's state.

---

# Tier Independence

Enchantments do not change the item's Tier.

Examples:

T6.4 remains Tier 6.

T8.1 remains Tier 8.

Tier and Enchantment are separate properties.

---

# Mastery Independence

Weapon Mastery determines whether the player may equip a weapon Tier.

Mastery does not automatically enchant equipment.

Enchanting an item does not grant Mastery XP unless explicitly defined by a separate system.

A player's Mastery and an item's Enchantment remain independent.

---

# Equipment Eligibility

The system must define which equipment categories are enchantable.

The architecture must support enchantments on:

- Weapons
- Off-Hands
- Helmets
- Chest Armor
- Boots
- Capes

V1 activation may be limited by configuration.

Very late-game progression is expected to focus primarily on weapons first.

Other equipment categories may be enabled later through Data Tables.

---

# Enchantment Permission

Each equipment definition contains an enchantment permission configuration.

Possible values include:

- EnchantmentDisabled
- EnchantmentEnabled
- EnchantmentLevelCap

Example:

Weapon A

- EnchantmentEnabled = true
- EnchantmentLevelCap = 4

Cape B

- EnchantmentEnabled = false
- EnchantmentLevelCap = 0

This avoids hardcoding equipment eligibility.

---

# Enchantment Costs

Every enchantment transition has its own cost.

The cost may include:

- Silver
- Common Enchantment Materials
- Rare Enchantment Materials
- Level-Specific Components
- Biome-Specific Components
- Boss Components
- Future Catalysts
- Future Service Fees

Each transition is balanced independently.

---

# Cost Scaling

Costs increase as enchantment level rises.

Scaling occurs in two ways:

- Higher quantities
- Greater material diversity

Later enchantment levels require more resources and rarer components.

# Multiple Resource Philosophy

The system does not rely on one universal enchantment material.

Multiple material categories are required to support:

- Several economic markets
- Different gameplay activities
- Biome relevance
- Boss farming
- Gathering specialization
- Long-term resource demand

---

# Persistent Material Relevance

Because enchantment progression is sequential, materials used for early enchantment levels remain permanently useful.

Every new item must pass through:

- .1
- .2
- .3
- .4

A player seeking a `.4` item still consumes the materials required for all previous stages.

This prevents lower-level enchantment resources from becoming obsolete.

---

# Material Sources

Enchantment materials may come from:

- Combat Loot
- Elite Enemies
- Bosses
- Biome Bosses
- Gathering
- Refining
- Salvaging
- Events
- Marketplace Trading
- Future Activities

The Enchantment System does not directly control resource generation.

It consumes resources defined by the economy and loot systems.

---

# Biome Integration

Enchanting may require components associated with specific biomes.

This helps preserve the relevance of older biomes.

Example structure:

- Forest Component
- Mountain Component
- Swamp Component
- Infernal Component

Exact resource assignments are defined elsewhere.

---

# Cycle Integration

Cycles may increase the acquisition rate of enchantment resources.

Cycles do not:

- Create new enchantment levels
- Change the `.4` maximum
- Automatically enchant equipment
- Increase an existing item's enchantment beyond `.4`

Cycle progression affects resource availability, not the structural enchantment cap.

---

# Enchantment Power

Each enchantment level applies a defined power modifier.

Possible affected values include:

- Item Power
- Base Damage
- Armor
- Magic Resistance
- Maximum Health
- Ability Scaling
- Secondary Stats

The Enchantment System does not hardcode which statistics are modified.

Each item or stat profile resolves its modifiers through data.

---

# Stat Calculation

Conceptual calculation:

Final Item Stats

=

Base Tier Stats

×

Quality Modifier

×

Enchantment Modifier

×

Other Valid Modifiers

The exact order of operations must be globally consistent.

The Stat System remains the authority for final calculations.

---

# Enchantment Modifier Model

Each enchantment level may define:

- Item Power Bonus
- Global Stat Multiplier
- Category-Specific Multiplier
- Individual Stat Modifiers
- Future Ability Modifiers

V1 should use the simplest model compatible with balancing needs.

All values remain data-driven.

---

# Ability Interaction

Enchantments primarily improve numerical equipment performance.

They do not automatically unlock new abilities.

An enchantment level may influence ability scaling only through existing item statistics or configured modifiers.

Enchantments do not change:

- Ability identity
- Ability slot
- Ability AI condition
- Ability activation behavior

Unless a future system explicitly defines an exception.

---

# Cape Interaction

If cape enchantments are enabled later, they may improve:

- Secondary statistics
- Passive magnitude
- Passive cooldown
- Passive duration

Cape enchantments must not change the cape's PassiveEffectID.

The identity of the cape remains unchanged.

---

# Upgrade Interface

The enchantment interface must display:

- Selected item
- Current enchantment level
- Next enchantment level
- Current stats
- Projected stats
- Required materials
- Owned material quantities
- Missing material quantities
- Silver cost
- Confirmation action

The result must be fully predictable before confirmation.

---

# Confirmation Flow

Recommended flow:

1. Player selects an item.
2. System identifies the next valid level.
3. System loads the upgrade recipe.
4. Interface displays exact costs.
5. Interface displays exact stat changes.
6. Player confirms.
7. Server validates all conditions.
8. Costs are consumed atomically.
9. Item enchantment level increases.
10. Derived stats are recalculated.
11. Inventory and equipment UI refresh.
12. Transaction is saved.

---

# Equipped Item Enchantment

An equipped item may be enchanted only if the implementation safely supports it.

Recommended V1 rule:

The player must unequip the item before enchanting it.

Advantages:

- Simpler validation
- Cleaner stat recalculation
- Lower risk of combat-state conflicts
- Easier transaction handling

The architecture may later support enchanting equipped items outside combat.

---

# Combat Restrictions

Enchanting is not allowed while:

- Combat is active
- An encounter is unresolved
- The item is reserved by another system
- The inventory is locked
- The player is in a restricted state
- A save transaction is incomplete

These restrictions prevent duplication and state desynchronization.

---

# Transaction Safety

Enchanting must be atomic.

The complete transaction either succeeds or does nothing.

The system must never allow a state where:

- Materials are consumed but the item is not upgraded.
- The item is upgraded but Silver is not consumed.
- The item is upgraded twice.
- The item disappears.
- The same request is processed multiple times.

---

# Idempotency

Every enchantment request should contain a unique transaction identifier.

Repeated processing of the same identifier must not apply the upgrade more than once.

This protects against:

- Network retries
- Double-clicks
- Client resubmissions
- Save interruptions

---

# Inventory Instance Identity

Enchantments apply to unique item instances.

Each enchantable item must have a persistent ItemInstanceID.

The system must not identify an enchanted item only through:

- Item type
- Tier
- Quality
- Inventory position

The unique instance is the authority.

---

# Item Stacking

Equipment with individual enchantment state cannot stack unless every persistent property is identical and the inventory architecture safely supports stacked instances.

Recommended V1 rule:

Enchantable equipment items do not stack.

Resources used for enchanting may stack.

---

# Market Integration

Enchanted items remain fully tradeable.

They are not:

- Soulbound
- Account-bound
- Character-bound
- Bind-on-equip

A player may sell any enchanted equipment item.

---

# Market Identity

The market must distinguish items by at least:

- ItemID
- Tier
- Enchantment Level
- Quality
- Durability State, if applicable
- Additional future modifiers

Examples:

- T8.2 Good Sword
- T8.2 Masterpiece Sword
- T8.4 Good Sword

These must be treated as distinct market products.

---

# Price Formation

The game does not impose a fixed market value for enchanted equipment.

The player economy determines price based on:

- Base item cost
- Quality
- Enchantment level
- Material cost
- Silver cost
- Demand
- Supply
- Build popularity
- Scarcity

---

# Silver Sink

Every upgrade may require Silver.

Silver costs should increase with enchantment level.

This creates a scalable endgame currency sink.

The exact curve is defined in data.

---

# Repair Interaction

Enchanting an item does not automatically repair it unless explicitly configured.

Recommended rule:

- Enchantment level changes.
- Current durability remains unchanged as a percentage.
- Maximum durability is recalculated if enchantment affects it.

Example:

An item at 60% durability remains at approximately 60% durability after enchanting.

This prevents enchantment from becoming a free repair mechanic.

---

# Salvage Interaction

If Salvaging is implemented later, enchanted items may return some enchantment materials.

The recovery rate must be below total invested cost.

The system must never allow a closed loop that generates infinite resources.

Possible recovery modifiers:

- Enchantment level
- Item Tier
- Item Quality
- Salvage Station
- Salvage Mastery

Salvage rules belong to a dedicated system.

---

# No Enchantment Reversal

V1 does not support reducing an item's enchantment level.

Players cannot convert:

T8.4

into

T8.3 + Materials

Enchantments are permanent upward investments.

A future disenchantment system would require its own design document and economy analysis.

---

# No Enchantment Reroll

Enchantments contain no random stat roll.

All items of the same:

- Item definition
- Tier
- Enchantment level
- Quality

receive the same deterministic enchantment modifiers unless another future system adds variable affixes.

---

# Offline Progression

If enchantment upgrades are instant in V1, no offline progression is required.

The architecture may support timed enchantment processes later.

A future timed process could include:

- Start timestamp
- Completion timestamp
- Queue position
- Station assignment
- Offline completion

V1 should remain instant unless another document explicitly enables timers.

---

# Validation Rules

The system must reject an upgrade when:

- The item does not exist.
- The item is not enchantable.
- The item is already .4.
- The requested level skips a stage.
- The recipe does not exist.
- Materials are insufficient.
- Silver is insufficient.
- The item is locked.
- The player is in combat.
- The transaction has already been processed.
- The save state cannot be secured.

# Edge Cases

## Maximum Level Item

Attempting to enchant a `.4` item returns:

Maximum Enchantment Reached

No cost is consumed.

---

## Item Sold During Interface Use

The final server validation fails.

No cost is consumed.

---

## Item Moved Between Inventory Slots

The operation still targets ItemInstanceID.

Inventory position is irrelevant.

---

## Double Confirmation

Only the first valid transaction is processed.

The repeated transaction identifier is ignored.

---

## Resource Quantity Changes

Costs are recalculated and revalidated at confirmation time.

The preview is not authoritative.

---

## Equipment Definition Disabled

Existing enchanted items remain valid.

New upgrades may be disabled through configuration.

---

## Tier Balance Update

Existing item identities remain unchanged.

Derived statistics are recalculated from current tables.

# Balancing Principles

The system should be balanced around total progression cost rather than success probability.

Key balancing variables include:

- Silver cost per stage
- Common material quantities
- Rare material quantities
- Tier multipliers
- Equipment category multipliers
- Resource acquisition rates
- Market supply
- Cycle reward scaling

---

# Cost Curve Philosophy

Recommended cost behavior:

.0 → .1

Accessible early upgrade

.1 → .2

Meaningful investment

.2 → .3

Major late-game investment

.3 → .4

Very late-game objective

The final transition should be expensive because of known costs, not random failure.

---

# Category Balance

Enchanting a Two-Handed weapon may cost more than enchanting a One-Handed weapon because it occupies two equipment slots.

Alternatively, category costs may be normalized through total power contribution.

This must remain configurable.

No category-specific formula should be hardcoded.

---

# Tier Balance

Higher-Tier items should generally require more enchantment resources than lower-Tier items.

Example principle:

T8.1 costs more than T4.1.

Exact scaling belongs to the EnchantmentTierCostModifierTable.

# Summary

The Enchantment System provides the primary vertical progression path for late-game and very late-game equipment.

All equipment begins at `.0` and advances sequentially through `.1`, `.2`, `.3`, and `.4`.

Every upgrade is guaranteed.

Difficulty comes from increasing Silver costs, larger material quantities, and progressively rarer resource requirements.

Enchantments are permanently attached to individual item instances and cannot be transferred, extracted, or skipped.

Quality, Tier, Mastery, and Enchantment remain fully independent.

The system creates long-term demand for equipment, resources, crafting, gathering, combat, and currency while avoiding random failure and infinite gear inflation.

The entire system is deterministic, transaction-safe, modular, and fully data-driven.
