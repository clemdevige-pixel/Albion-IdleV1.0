# 36. VENDOR SYSTEM

## Purpose

The Vendor System defines how players purchase basic goods from NPC merchants and sell specific low-value item categories for Silver.

NPC Vendors provide accessibility, convenience, and economic fallback services.

They are not intended to replace:

- Gathering
- Refining
- Crafting
- Loot
- The future player-driven Market System

The Vendor System must support the early game without becoming the primary source of valuable equipment or resources.

---

# Design Goals

The Vendor System must:

- Prevent players from becoming permanently blocked.
- Provide access to essential starter goods.
- Create predictable Silver sinks.
- Convert selected low-value items into Silver.
- Avoid competing with crafting.
- Avoid competing with the future player market.
- Use fixed, data-driven prices.
- Support multiple vendor identities and inventories.
- Remain simple to understand.

---

# Core Philosophy

NPC Vendors are utility services.

They exist to:

- Help new players begin progressing.
- Replace essential low-level tools.
- Supply basic consumables.
- Purchase selected low-value goods.
- Provide stable and predictable transactions.

They must never become the optimal source of high-value progression.

---

# Vendor Roles

NPC Vendors may perform one or both of the following roles:

- Sell approved items to the player.
- Buy approved items from the player.

A vendor may be configured as:

- Buy Only
- Sell Only
- Buy and Sell
- Service Only

---

# Vendor Types

The architecture supports multiple vendor types.

Possible examples include:

- General Merchant
- Tool Merchant
- Food Merchant
- Potion Merchant
- Equipment Merchant
- Junk Collector
- Trophy Buyer
- Island Merchant

V1 may use a small number of generalized merchants.

---

# Vendor Inventory Philosophy

Vendors sell only basic goods.

Their inventory should focus on:

- Accessibility
- Starter recovery
- Convenience
- Common consumables

Vendor inventory must not include high-value progression items.

---

# Items Vendors May Sell

Approved categories may include:

- Tier 1 Gathering Tools
- Tier 2 Gathering Tools
- Basic Food
- Common Potions
- Standard Consumables
- Low-Tier Emergency Equipment
- Universal Basic Components
- Tutorial Items
- Starter Supplies

Exact inventories are defined through Data Tables.

---

# Items Vendors Must Not Sell

NPC Vendors do not sell:

- High-Tier Equipment
- High-Quality Equipment
- Enchanted Equipment
- Rare Resources
- Refined Rare Materials
- Boss Loot
- Enchantment Materials
- Exclusive Equipment
- Optimized Builds
- Endgame Consumables
- Player-Crafted Premium Goods

---

# Crafting Protection

Vendor goods must never invalidate crafting.

To protect crafting:

- Vendor equipment remains low Tier.
- Vendor equipment uses basic Quality.
- Vendor equipment is never enchanted.
- Vendor prices are not lower than intended crafting progression costs.
- Advanced goods remain unavailable from NPCs.

The best progression items must come from gameplay systems.

---

# Vendor Equipment Rules

When NPC Vendors sell equipment, the following default rules apply:

- Low Tier only
- Normal Quality
- Enchantment level .0
- No random affixes
- No special passive variants
- Fixed item definitions

Vendor equipment is intended for recovery and early progression.

---

# Vendor Purchase Transactions

A player may purchase an item when:

- The vendor sells the item.
- The requested quantity is valid.
- The player has enough Silver.
- The player has enough inventory capacity.
- The item is currently enabled.
- The transaction is authorized.

---

# Purchase Flow

1. Player interacts with a vendor.
2. Vendor interface opens.
3. Player selects an item.
4. Player chooses a quantity.
5. Total Silver cost is calculated.
6. Inventory capacity is validated.
7. Player confirms.
8. Silver is removed.
9. Item is added to inventory.
10. Transaction is saved.
11. Interface refreshes.

---

# Purchase Cost

Conceptual formula:

Total Purchase Cost

=

Unit Buy Price

×

Quantity

Vendor prices are fixed.

No dynamic pricing is used in V1.

---

# Vendor Buyback Role

NPC Vendors may purchase selected item categories from players.

This provides:

- Inventory cleanup
- A stable Silver source
- Value for junk items
- Value for designated trophies
- A fallback for low-value goods

NPC buyback must not become the best way to earn Silver.

---

# Items Vendors May Buy

Approved buyback categories may include:

- Common Equipment
- Basic Tools
- Basic Food
- Common Potions
- Standard Consumables
- Junk Items
- Vendor Trophies
- Items explicitly marked as VendorSellable

Each item must be individually or categorically approved.

---

# Items Vendors Must Not Buy

NPC Vendors do not purchase:

- Gathering Resources
- Refined Materials
- Rare Resources
- Rare Components
- Enchanted Equipment
- Boss Items
- Boss Materials
- Enchantment Materials
- Exclusive Items
- Strategic Economy Goods
- Future Market-Focused Goods

---

# Junk Items

Junk Items are objects primarily intended to be sold to NPC Vendors.

Examples may include:

- Broken Weapons
- Damaged Relics
- Monster Teeth
- Cracked Idols
- Old Coins
- Worthless Artifacts
- Ruined Equipment Parts

Names are illustrative only.

Junk Items may:

- Drop from enemies.
- Drop from bosses.
- Be granted by exploration.
- Have no other gameplay function.
- Possess a fixed vendor value.

---

# Trophy Items

Trophy Items may be valuable enemy drops intended for sale.

They differ from Junk Items by representing:

- Rare enemy trophies
- Elite trophies
- Boss trophies
- Biome-specific trophies

Trophies may have higher fixed vendor prices.

A Trophy Item must not also be required for progression unless explicitly configured.

---

# Common Equipment Buyback

Vendors may purchase common equipment only when configured.

Recommended default:

- Normal Quality accepted
- Low-Tier equipment accepted
- Enchanted equipment rejected
- Rare or special equipment rejected

This allows players to clear basic drops without creating an artificial market floor for valuable items.

---

# Sell Transaction

A player may sell an item when:

- The item exists.
- The vendor accepts its category.
- The item is not locked.
- The item is not equipped.
- The item is not reserved by another system.
- The quantity is valid.
- The item has a defined Vendor Sell Price.

---

# Sell Flow

1. Player selects an item.
2. Vendor validates eligibility.
3. Vendor displays the unit sell price.
4. Player selects a quantity.
5. Total Silver return is calculated.
6. Player confirms.
7. Item quantity is removed.
8. Silver is added to the wallet.
9. Transaction is saved.
10. Interface refreshes.

---

# Sell Value

Conceptual formula:

Total Sell Value

=

Unit Vendor Sell Price

×

Quantity

Prices remain fixed.

---

# Buy Price and Sell Price

Each sellable vendor item may define:

- Vendor Buy Price
- Vendor Sell Price

Definitions:

Vendor Buy Price:

The amount of Silver the player pays to purchase the item from an NPC.

Vendor Sell Price:

The amount of Silver the player receives when selling the item to an NPC.

---

# Price Difference

Vendor Sell Price should normally be lower than Vendor Buy Price.

Example:

Vendor Buy Price:

100 Silver

Vendor Sell Price:

20 Silver

This prevents infinite resale loops.

---

# Anti-Arbitrage Rule

The system must never allow:

Purchase From Vendor

↓

Sell To Vendor

↓

Earn More Silver

For every item:

Vendor Sell Price

<

Vendor Buy Price

When both prices exist.

---

# Fixed Pricing

All NPC Vendor prices are fixed.

Prices do not change based on:

- City
- Biome
- Player population
- Supply
- Demand
- Time
- Stock
- Events
- Player reputation

Any future exception requires explicit configuration.

---

# No Dynamic Economy

NPC Vendors do not simulate an economy.

They do not:

- Raise prices when demand increases.
- Lower prices when supply increases.
- Run out of standard goods.
- Compete with other vendors.
- React to player purchases.

Dynamic pricing belongs to the future Market System.

---

# Vendor Stock

Recommended V1 rule:

Standard vendor inventory has unlimited stock.

This applies to:

- Starter tools
- Basic food
- Common consumables
- Emergency low-level equipment

Limited-stock vendors may be added later.

---

# Unlimited Stock Philosophy

Unlimited stock prevents essential progression items from becoming unavailable.

Because NPCs sell only basic goods, unlimited supply does not threaten advanced crafting.

---

# Vendor Refresh

Standard V1 vendors do not require stock refresh timers.

Their inventory is loaded from Data Tables.

Future limited-stock vendors may support:

- Daily refresh
- Timed refresh
- Account-specific stock
- Global stock

These features are excluded from V1.

---

# Vendor Location

Vendors may be located in:

- Cities
- Tutorial Areas
- Player Island
- Safe Hubs
- Future Settlements

Vendor access may be restricted by area or progression.

---

# City Vendors

Cities may contain several specialized vendors.

Example structure:

General Merchant:

- Starter equipment
- Basic tools

Consumable Merchant:

- Food
- Potions

Collector:

- Junk items
- Trophies

Specialization is optional and primarily affects presentation.

---

# Player Island Vendors

The Player Island may support vendors after specific upgrades.

Island vendors may offer:

- Basic supplies
- Convenience purchasing
- Junk item buyback

They use the same fixed-price rules.

Island vendor access may require:

- Building construction
- Building upgrade
- Worker assignment
- Progression unlock

Exact requirements belong to the Player Island System.

---

# Vendor Access Rules

A vendor may define:

- Required Player Progression
- Required Island Level
- Required Quest
- Required Biome Unlock
- Required Building
- Required Feature Flag

These rules remain data-driven.

---

# Currency Integration

All vendor transactions use Silver.

V1 does not support:

- Barter
- Tokens
- Premium Currency
- Faction Currency
- Mixed Payments

The Currency System is authoritative for all payments.

---

# Inventory Integration

Purchased goods are added to the player inventory.

The Vendor System must validate:

- Available slots
- Stack capacity
- Item stack rules
- Unique item constraints

If the purchase cannot fit:

- The transaction fails.
- No Silver is consumed.

---

# Equipment Integration

Equipment sold by NPCs uses the standard Item System.

Vendor equipment is not a special item class.

It still possesses:

- ItemInstanceID
- ItemDefinitionID
- Tier
- Quality
- Enchantment
- Durability

---

# Durability of Vendor Equipment

Recommended rule:

New equipment purchased from a vendor starts at full durability.

Example:

Current Durability

=

Maximum Durability

Used equipment is not sold by vendors in V1.

---

# Quality of Vendor Equipment

Vendor equipment uses a fixed Quality.

Recommended V1 default:

Normal Quality

Vendors do not roll random Quality.

---

# Enchantment of Vendor Equipment

All vendor equipment is sold at enchantment level `.0`.

NPC Vendors do not sell enchanted items.

---

# Item Binding

Vendor items are not bound.

They follow the same ownership and trade rules as equivalent gameplay items.

Future market restrictions may be defined separately.

---

# Selling Damaged Equipment

Vendors may accept damaged equipment if the item category is eligible.

The sell price may optionally depend on durability.

Recommended V1 rule:

Sell value scales with current durability percentage.

Conceptual formula:

Adjusted Sell Value

=

Base Vendor Sell Price

×

Current Durability Percentage

---

# Zero Durability Equipment

Equipment reaching zero durability is destroyed.

It cannot be sold to a vendor.

No transaction is possible because the item no longer exists.

---

# Quality Sell Modifier

If multiple Quality levels are accepted by a vendor, the sell value may use a Quality modifier.

Recommended V1 simplification:

Only configured basic Quality equipment is accepted.

This avoids creating a vendor price floor for valuable Quality items.

---

# Enchantment Sell Restriction

Enchanted equipment is not accepted by NPC Vendors in V1.

This prevents players from converting valuable progression investments into fixed-price Silver.

Enchanted items should be:

- Used
- Stored
- Salvaged in a future system
- Sold through the future player market

---

# Bulk Selling

The Vendor Interface should support:

- Sell Selected
- Sell Stack
- Sell All Junk

Sell All Junk automatically selects items marked as Junk.

It must not select other categories.

---

# Sell All Junk Safety

The system must never infer junk status from item value.

Only items explicitly configured with:

ItemTag = Junk

may be included.

This prevents accidental sale of valuable goods.

---

# Vendor Interface

The Vendor Interface should display:

- Vendor Name
- Vendor Type
- Available Inventory
- Item Icon
- Item Name
- Tier
- Quantity Selector
- Unit Price
- Total Price
- Player Silver
- Inventory Capacity
- Buy Tab
- Sell Tab

---

# Purchase Preview

Before confirmation, the interface displays:

- Item
- Quantity
- Unit Price
- Total Price
- Current Silver
- Remaining Silver
- Inventory result

The player must know the exact result.

---

# Sale Preview

Before confirmation, the interface displays:

- Selected item
- Quantity
- Unit sell value
- Total Silver received
- Durability modifier, if applicable
- Confirmation action

---

# Item Tooltips

Vendor tooltips use standard Item System tooltips.

They may additionally display:

- Vendor Buy Price
- Vendor Sell Price
- Vendor Eligibility
- Purchase Restriction

---

# Transaction Safety

Vendor transactions must be atomic.

For purchases:

Either:

- Silver is consumed.
- Item is granted.

or:

Nothing changes.

For sales:

Either:

- Item is removed.
- Silver is granted.

or:

Nothing changes.

---

# Idempotency

Every vendor transaction receives a unique TransactionID.

Repeated requests using the same identifier must not be processed twice.

This prevents:

- Duplicate purchases
- Duplicate sales
- Double-click exploits
- Network retry exploits

---

# Server Authority

All vendor logic is authoritative.

The client may:

- Display inventory
- Display prices
- Request purchases
- Request sales

The client may not:

- Define prices
- Generate items
- Generate Silver
- Change accepted categories
- Complete transactions directly

---

# Vendor Inventory Data

Vendor inventories are defined through Data Tables.

They are not manually embedded in NPC scripts.

This allows:

- Balance changes
- Vendor variants
- Event configuration
- Localization
- Content expansion

# Validation Rules

A purchase fails when:

- Vendor does not exist.
- Vendor is disabled.
- Player lacks access.
- Item is not sold by the vendor.
- Quantity is invalid.
- Silver is insufficient.
- Inventory capacity is insufficient.
- Transaction has already been processed.

A sale fails when:

- Vendor does not buy items.
- Item is not accepted.
- Item does not exist.
- Item is equipped.
- Item is locked.
- Quantity is invalid.
- Item is enchanted and enchanted items are rejected.
- Item belongs to an excluded category.
- Transaction has already been processed.

---

# Edge Cases

## Inventory Full During Purchase

The transaction fails.

No Silver is consumed.

---

## Item Removed Before Sale Confirmation

Final validation fails.

No Silver is granted.

---

## Price Data Changes While Interface Is Open

The authoritative price is recalculated at confirmation.

The interface refreshes if the price changed.

---

## Double Purchase Request

Only the first transaction is processed.

---

## Selling a Partial Stack

Only the selected quantity is removed.

Remaining items stay in the inventory.

---

## Vendor Disabled Through Configuration

The vendor becomes unavailable.

Existing items purchased from that vendor remain valid.

---

## Item Removed From Vendor Inventory

New purchases are blocked.

Previously purchased items remain unaffected.

---

# Balancing Principles

Vendor balancing should focus on:

- Avoiding progression bypasses
- Protecting crafting value
- Maintaining meaningful Silver sinks
- Preventing profitable resale loops
- Keeping starter recovery affordable
- Limiting vendor-generated Silver

---

# Purchase Price Philosophy

Vendor purchase prices should be:

- Affordable enough for recovery
- Expensive enough to preserve crafting
- Predictable
- Stable
- Independent of player demand

---

# Buyback Price Philosophy

Vendor sell values should be conservative.

They should:

- Give junk a purpose
- Help inventory cleanup
- Provide small Silver income
- Never outperform primary progression activities
- Never define the value of strategic goods

# Summary

The Vendor System provides predictable NPC-based buying and selling services.

NPC Vendors sell only basic items such as starter tools, common consumables, and low-level emergency equipment.

They do not sell rare, enchanted, high-quality, or endgame goods.

Vendors may purchase only approved categories such as junk items, trophies, common consumables, tools, and selected basic equipment.

They do not buy strategic resources, rare materials, enchanted equipment, boss items, or future market-focused goods.

All prices are fixed and defined through Data Tables.

Vendor transactions use Silver, are server-authoritative, atomic, deterministic, and protected against duplication and arbitrage.

The system supports early-game accessibility and inventory cleanup while preserving the importance of gathering, crafting, loot, and the future player-driven economy.
