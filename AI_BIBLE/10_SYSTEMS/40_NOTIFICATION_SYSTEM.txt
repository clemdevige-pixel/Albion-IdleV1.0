# 40. NOTIFICATION SYSTEM

## Purpose

The Notification System communicates important gameplay events to the player.

It supports:

- Temporary in-game messages
- Persistent notifications
- Notifications generated while the player is disconnected
- Grouped login summaries
- Actionable navigation toward the relevant game system

The system does not send mobile, desktop, email, or external push notifications in V1.

---

# Design Goals

The Notification System must:

- Inform players without interrupting gameplay excessively.
- Preserve important information.
- Avoid notification spam.
- Support background progression results.
- Group accumulated notifications at login.
- Provide direct access to relevant interfaces.
- Remain generic and system-independent.
- Support future gameplay systems.
- Be fully server-authoritative for persistent notifications.

---

# Core Philosophy

Notifications should communicate meaningful changes.

They should not become a constant stream of low-value messages.

The system distinguishes between:

- Temporary feedback
- Persistent information
- Grouped login summaries

Each notification type must use the least intrusive presentation appropriate to its importance.

---

# Supported Channels

V1 supports only in-game notification channels.

Supported channels include:

- Toast Notifications
- Notification Center
- Login Summary
- Interface Badges
- Contextual Highlights

---

# Excluded Channels

V1 does not support:

- Mobile Push Notifications
- Desktop Push Notifications
- Email Notifications
- SMS Notifications
- Browser Notifications
- External Messaging Services

---

# Notification Categories

Notifications are divided into two main persistence categories:

- Temporary Notifications
- Persistent Notifications

---

# Temporary Notifications

Temporary Notifications provide immediate feedback.

They appear briefly and disappear automatically.

They are not stored in the Notification Center.

Examples include:

- Silver received
- Item collected
- Craft started
- Refining started
- Worker assigned
- Equipment changed
- Resource gathered
- Purchase completed
- Sale completed

---

# Temporary Notification Philosophy

Temporary notifications should:

- Confirm immediate player actions.
- Remain concise.
- Avoid blocking input.
- Avoid accumulating.
- Avoid requiring manual dismissal.

They should not be used for information the player may need later.

---

# Toast Notifications

Temporary notifications are generally displayed as toasts.

A toast may contain:

- Icon
- Short title
- Short message
- Quantity
- Duration
- Optional visual emphasis

---

# Toast Duration

Toast duration is data-driven.

Recommended default ranges:

- Minor feedback: 2 seconds
- Standard feedback: 3 seconds
- Important temporary feedback: 5 seconds

Critical information should normally use a persistent notification instead.

---

# Toast Queue

Multiple toasts may enter a display queue.

The system should avoid covering large portions of the interface.

Recommended behavior:

- Display a limited number simultaneously.
- Queue additional messages.
- Merge compatible messages.
- Discard redundant low-value messages when necessary.

---

# Toast Aggregation

Repeated temporary notifications may be grouped.

Example:

Instead of displaying:

- 1 Wood collected
- 1 Wood collected
- 1 Wood collected
- 1 Wood collected

The system may display:

- 4 Wood collected

Aggregation rules are configured by notification type.

---

# Persistent Notifications

Persistent Notifications contain information that remains relevant after the initial event.

They are stored in the Notification Center.

Examples include:

- Craft completed
- Refining completed
- Worker task completed
- Building construction completed
- Island upgrade completed
- New Tier unlocked
- New recipe unlocked
- New Weapon Mastery unlocked
- Important system warning

---

# Persistent Notification States

A persistent notification may have the following states:

- Unread
- Read
- Archived
- Expired
- Deleted

---

# Unread State

A notification is Unread when:

- It has not been opened.
- It has not been included in an acknowledged summary.
- Its related action has not been triggered through another interface, unless configured otherwise.

Unread notifications contribute to Notification Center badges.

---

# Read State

A notification becomes Read when:

- The player opens it.
- The player clicks its action.
- The player explicitly marks it as read.
- The login summary is acknowledged, if configured to mark included notifications as read.

---

# Archived State

Archived notifications remain stored but are removed from the default active list.

The player may view archived notifications through a dedicated filter.

Archiving does not execute the notification action.

---

# Expired State

Some notifications may automatically expire.

Examples:

- Temporary event announcement
- Limited-time warning
- Outdated system information

Expiration behavior is defined per notification type.

---

# Deleted State

Deleted notifications are removed from the player's visible history.

System-required audit data may remain stored separately.

---

# Notification Center

The Notification Center stores persistent notifications.

It acts as the central interface for important gameplay information.

---

# Notification Center Interface

The interface should display:

- Notification icon
- Notification title
- Short message
- Category
- Timestamp
- Read state
- Priority
- Optional action indicator
- Optional expiration time

---

# Notification Center Filters

Recommended filters include:

- All
- Unread
- Crafting
- Refining
- Workers
- Island
- Progression
- System

Future categories may be added without changing the core architecture.

---

# Notification Center Sorting

Recommended default order:

Newest First

Optional sorting modes may include:

- Oldest First
- Priority
- Category
- Unread First

---

# Notification Center Badge

The main notification icon displays the number of unread persistent notifications.

The badge may be capped visually.

Example:

- 1
- 8
- 24
- 99+

The actual unread count remains available internally.

---

# Login Notifications

Events that occur while the player is disconnected are stored by the server.

At the next login, they are presented through a grouped summary.

---

# Login Summary

The Login Summary groups accumulated notifications by category.

Example:

Welcome back!

During your absence:

- 8 Crafts completed
- 5 Refining jobs completed
- 3 Workers finished their tasks
- 2 Buildings completed
- 1 Island upgrade became available

---

# Login Summary Philosophy

The Login Summary must:

- Avoid displaying one notification per completed event.
- Present the most important information first.
- Remain easy to dismiss.
- Provide direct navigation.
- Preserve detailed notifications in the Notification Center.

---

# Login Summary Generation

At login:

1. Background Progression is resolved.
2. Participating systems generate notifications.
3. Persistent notifications are stored.
4. Notifications are grouped by summary category.
5. The Login Summary is displayed.
6. The updated notification state is saved.

---

# Summary Grouping

Notifications may be grouped using:

- Notification Type
- Source System
- Source Entity
- Completion Category
- Priority

Example grouping key:

CraftCompleted

All compatible CraftCompleted notifications may become:

12 Crafts completed

---

# Summary Detail

Each summary line may provide:

- Total event count
- Total item count
- Category icon
- Most important event
- Direct action

Detailed individual notifications remain available in the Notification Center.

---

# Summary Interaction

The player may:

- Open a grouped category
- Open the Notification Center
- Dismiss the summary
- Mark included notifications as read
- Leave included notifications unread

Exact read behavior is configurable.

---

# Actionable Notifications

Persistent notifications may contain an optional action.

The action allows the player to navigate directly to the related system.

---

# Action Examples

Craft Completed:

- Open Crafting Interface
- Select Crafting Station
- Highlight Completed Output

Worker Task Completed:

- Open Worker Interface
- Select Worker
- Highlight Reward

Building Completed:

- Open Player Island
- Select Building
- Highlight Completed Construction

Recipe Unlocked:

- Open Crafting Interface
- Select Recipe
- Highlight New Entry

Mastery Unlocked:

- Open Mastery Interface
- Select Weapon Family
- Highlight New Rank

---

# Action Structure

A notification action may contain:

- ActionType
- ActionTargetID
- ActionSubTargetID
- ActionParameters
- FallbackActionType

---

# Action Types

Supported generic action types may include:

- OpenInterface
- OpenInterfaceTab
- SelectEntity
- HighlightEntity
- OpenItem
- OpenRecipe
- OpenBuilding
- OpenWorker
- OpenMastery
- OpenZone
- NoAction

---

# Action Independence

The Notification System does not implement destination logic.

It only publishes a navigation request.

The relevant UI or gameplay system handles the action.

---

# Invalid Actions

An action may become invalid.

Examples:

- Building was removed.
- Worker no longer exists.
- Item was already collected.
- Recipe was disabled.
- Destination interface is unavailable.

When an action is invalid:

- The notification remains readable.
- The system attempts its fallback action.
- If no fallback exists, no navigation occurs.
- No error is shown unless useful to the player.

---

# Action Fallback

Example:

Primary Action:

Open specific Crafting Station and select output.

Fallback Action:

Open Crafting Interface.

This ensures notifications remain useful even when their exact target is unavailable.

---

# Notification Priority

Notifications may use priorities such as:

- Low
- Normal
- High
- Critical

---

# Low Priority

Examples:

- Minor informational update
- Non-essential unlock reminder

Low-priority notifications may be silently added to the Notification Center.

---

# Normal Priority

Examples:

- Craft completed
- Worker completed
- Refining completed

These appear in standard summaries and persistent lists.

---

# High Priority

Examples:

- Important unlock
- Building completed
- Progression milestone
- Account warning

High-priority notifications may receive stronger visual emphasis.

---

# Critical Priority

Examples:

- Account security issue
- Save recovery warning
- Required maintenance notice

Critical notifications may require explicit acknowledgement.

Gameplay events should rarely use Critical priority.

---

# Notification Sources

Notifications may be created by:

- Crafting System
- Refining System
- Worker System
- Player Island System
- Progression System
- Equipment System
- Vendor System
- Save System
- Authentication System
- Future Market System
- Future Guild System
- Future Event System

---

# Source Ownership

The originating system defines:

- Why the notification exists
- Notification type
- Source entity
- Message parameters
- Suggested action
- Persistence
- Priority

The Notification System handles:

- Storage
- Grouping
- Delivery
- State
- Presentation requests

---

# Notification Creation Flow

Gameplay Event

↓

Source System Builds Notification Request

↓

Notification Service Validates Request

↓

Notification Created

↓

Stored if Persistent

↓

Delivered if Player Online

↓

Queued for Login Summary if Player Offline

---

# Online Delivery

When the player is online:

Temporary Notification:

↓

Display immediately as toast.

Persistent Notification:

↓

Store in Notification Center.

↓

Optionally display toast or banner.

---

# Offline Delivery

When the player is offline:

Temporary notifications are normally discarded.

Persistent notifications are stored.

At the next login, eligible notifications are included in the Login Summary.

---

# Offline Event Rule

Only events classified as meaningful and persistent should generate offline notifications.

The system should not reconstruct every minor background event.

Example:

A queue containing 100 completed craft units may create:

- One persistent completion notification
- One grouped summary entry

It should not create 100 separate notifications unless explicitly required.

---

# Deduplication

The system prevents duplicate notifications.

Each notification request may include a unique EventID.

If the same EventID is processed twice:

Only one notification is created.

---

# Notification Identity

Every persistent notification has a unique NotificationID.

The NotificationID is used for:

- State changes
- Actions
- Archiving
- Deletion
- Deduplication
- Audit logs

---

# Aggregation Key

Notifications may define an AggregationKey.

Compatible notifications with the same key may be merged.

Example:

AggregationKey:

CraftCompleted:StationID

This may group all completed crafts from one station.

---

# Aggregation Rules

Aggregation may combine:

- Event Count
- Item Quantity
- Source Count
- Earliest Timestamp
- Latest Timestamp
- Related Entity IDs

The original event references may remain stored if detailed history is required.

---

# Persistent Storage

Persistent notifications are stored server-side.

The client may cache notification data but is not authoritative.

---

# Save System Integration

Persistent notifications are linked to the player's account and save state.

Recommended architecture:

- Notification records stored in a dedicated server collection.
- Notification metadata referenced by the player account.
- Read and archive states persisted server-side.

Notifications do not need to be embedded directly inside the full gameplay snapshot if a dedicated notification store is used.

---

# Authentication Integration

Notifications belong to the Player Account.

They are accessible only after authentication.

A Guest Account receives notifications normally.

When the Guest Account is linked:

Its notifications remain attached to the same Player Account.

---

# Background Progression Integration

The Background Progression System does not generate notification text directly.

Instead:

1. It resolves passive systems.
2. Each passive system reports completed events.
3. Each source system creates notifications.
4. The Notification System groups them.
5. The Login Summary displays them.

---

# Localization

Notification content must be localizable.

Stored notifications should avoid saving fully rendered text when possible.

Recommended stored format:

- LocalizationKey
- LocalizationParameters

Example:

LocalizationKey:

Notification.Crafting.Completed

Parameters:

- ItemName
- Quantity
- StationName

---

# Historical Localization

If the player changes language:

Previously stored notifications should display in the new language when possible.

This requires storing localization keys and parameters instead of final text.

---

# Notification Center Actions

The player may:

- Open Notification
- Execute Notification Action
- Mark as Read
- Mark as Unread
- Archive
- Delete
- Mark All as Read
- Archive All Read Notifications

---

# Bulk Actions

Recommended bulk actions include:

- Mark All as Read
- Archive All Read
- Clear Expired
- Filter by Category

Deleting all notifications should require confirmation.

---

# Read Synchronization

Read state is stored server-side.

If the player uses another device:

The same read and archive states are restored.

---

# Pagination

The Notification Center should use pagination or incremental loading.

The system should not load the entire notification history at once.

Recommended default:

- 20 to 50 notifications per page

---

# Retention

Persistent notifications do not remain indefinitely unless required.

Retention is defined per notification type.

Recommended examples:

- Standard gameplay completion: 30 days
- Important progression unlock: 90 days
- Critical account message: until acknowledged or manually resolved

Exact durations are balance and infrastructure settings.

---

# History Limits

The system may impose a maximum stored notification count per player.

When the limit is reached:

1. Expired notifications are removed first.
2. Archived notifications are removed second.
3. Oldest read notifications are removed third.
4. Unread high-priority notifications are preserved.

---

# Notification Spam Prevention

The system must prevent excessive notifications.

Strategies include:

- Aggregation
- Rate limiting
- Category cooldowns
- Duplicate suppression
- Priority filtering
- Toast queue limits

---

# Category Rate Limiting

A category may limit temporary notifications.

Example:

Maximum one resource toast every 0.5 seconds.

Additional events are aggregated.

Persistent gameplay events should not be silently lost.

---

# Notification Settings

V1 may provide simple in-game notification preferences.

Possible configurable options:

- Enable or disable temporary loot toasts
- Enable or disable Silver toasts
- Enable or disable minor progression toasts
- Control sound effects
- Control Notification Center badges

Important persistent notifications cannot be fully disabled if they are required for gameplay clarity.

Detailed settings belong to the Settings System.

---

# Audio Feedback

Notifications may trigger optional sound cues.

Sound selection is defined by presentation profile.

Examples:

- Standard completion sound
- Important unlock sound
- Warning sound

Audio respects global and notification-specific settings.

---

# Visual Accessibility

Notifications must not rely only on color.

They should also use:

- Icons
- Labels
- Shapes
- Priority indicators
- Text

Animations should respect reduced-motion settings when available.

---

# Transaction Safety

Persistent notification creation should be atomic with the related gameplay event when necessary.

Example:

Craft completes

↓

Craft output stored

↓

Persistent notification created

↓

Save committed

If notification creation fails, the gameplay reward must remain valid.

The notification may be reconstructed from event logs if required.

---

# Reliability Priority

Gameplay state is always more important than notification state.

A missing notification must never cause:

- Missing rewards
- Lost progression
- Repeated rewards
- Failed crafting completion

Notifications communicate state; they do not own it.

---

# Idempotency

Notification creation requests use EventID or another idempotency key.

Repeated processing must not create duplicate persistent notifications.

---

# Security

The server validates all persistent notifications.

The client cannot:

- Create notifications
- Change notification rewards
- Mark another player's notifications
- Modify action targets
- Change stored timestamps
- Increase notification priority

---

# Analytics

Recommended analytics include:

- Notifications created by type
- Notifications opened
- Action click rate
- Login summary engagement
- Time before reading
- Archive rate
- Expiration rate
- Toast aggregation rate
- Unread notification count
- Notification Center visits

---

# Validation Rules

A notification request fails when:

- Notification type does not exist.
- Notification type is disabled.
- Player account does not exist.
- Required localization parameters are missing.
- Action data is invalid.
- EventID has already been processed.
- Source system is unauthorized.

---

# Edge Cases

## Player Reconnects Multiple Times

Previously processed background events do not generate duplicate notifications.

---

## Notification Action Target Removed

The fallback action is used.

---

## Notification Expires While Interface Is Open

The interface refreshes and removes or archives it according to configuration.

---

## Player Changes Language

Stored localization keys are rendered using the current language.

---

## Extremely Long Absence

Notifications are grouped.

The Login Summary shows category totals rather than every individual event.

Retention and history rules still apply.

---

## Empty Login Summary

No summary is shown.

The player enters the game normally.

---

## Notification Center Unavailable

Persistent notification data remains stored.

The player may access it later.

---

## Client Disconnects Before Reading Summary

Notifications remain unread and may appear again at the next login according to summary rules.

# Summary

The Notification System provides clear, non-intrusive communication for gameplay events.

V1 uses only in-game notifications. Minor feedback appears as temporary toasts, while important events are stored as persistent notifications in the Notification Center.

Events completed while the player is disconnected are saved by the server and grouped into a concise Login Summary at the next connection. Individual details remain available in the Notification Center.

Notifications may contain generic actions that open the relevant interface, select a target, or highlight a specific item, worker, recipe, building, or progression entry.

The system is server-authoritative, localized, data-driven, deduplicated, actionable, extensible, and designed to avoid spam while preserving important information.
