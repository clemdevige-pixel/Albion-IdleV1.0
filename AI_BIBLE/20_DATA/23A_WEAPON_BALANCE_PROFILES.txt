# 23A. WEAPON BALANCE PROFILES

---

# Purpose

Weapon Balance Profiles define what each weapon is intended to do well before numerical balancing begins.

They exist to prevent invalid comparisons between weapons with different purposes.

A weapon designed to kill bosses must not be considered weak simply because it produces less Fame per hour than a dedicated farming weapon.

A weapon designed for dungeon consistency must not be forced toward the same burst or single-target output as a boss killer.

Profiles are design and benchmark metadata only.

They never grant hidden combat bonuses.

---

# Two-Axis Profile

Every weapon specialization is described through two complementary axes.

## Gameplay Profile

Describes how the weapon expresses its power.

Examples include:

- Bruiser / Execute
- Sustain / Burst
- Control / DPS
- DoT / Ramp-up
- Burst / Control
- Combo / Execute

This vocabulary is descriptive and extensible.

It is not a closed runtime enum that limits future weapon design.

## Content Role

Describes where the weapon is expected to create the most value.

Current useful roles include:

- General Progression
- Fame Farm
- Boss
- Dungeon

Future content may introduce new roles without changing the underlying principle.

Each weapon has one Primary Content Role and may have one Secondary Content Role.

---

# Core Balance Rule

Profiles describe expected performance; they do not directly modify performance.

There must never be logic such as:

- +X% damage against bosses because primaryRole = Boss
- +X% Fame because primaryRole = Fame Farm
- hidden dungeon multipliers derived from the profile

The role must emerge naturally from existing authored weapon data:

- base damage
- attack speed
- ability ratios
- cooldowns
- damage-over-time
- execute conditions
- control effects
- setup requirements
- handling and off-hand access

# Current Weapon Assignments

## Broadsword

Gameplay Profile:

Bruiser / Execute

Primary Content Role:

General Progression

Secondary Content Role:

Boss

Balance Expectation:

Reliable all-round progression weapon with strong finishing pressure. It should be safe and consistent without becoming the best Fame farmer or the strongest dedicated boss killer.

---

## Longbow

Gameplay Profile:

Sustain / Burst

Primary Content Role:

Fame Farm

Secondary Content Role:

General Progression

Balance Expectation:

Efficient repeatable PvE clear with low downtime. It should convert ordinary world encounters into strong Fame/hour without matching dedicated boss weapons on long single-target fights.

---

## Badon

Gameplay Profile:

Control / DPS

Primary Content Role:

Dungeon

Secondary Content Role:

General Progression

Balance Expectation:

Control-oriented PvE weapon that gains value across chained or dangerous encounters. It should be particularly comfortable in dungeons without leading pure Fame/hour or boss-DPS benchmarks.

---

## Infernal Staff

Gameplay Profile:

DoT / Ramp-up

Primary Content Role:

Boss

Secondary Content Role:

Dungeon

Balance Expectation:

Sustained damage weapon rewarded by targets that live long enough for burns and setup to pay off. Its relative efficiency should improve with encounter duration and fall on very short farming fights.

---

## Spiked Gauntlets

Gameplay Profile:

Burst / Control

Primary Content Role:

Fame Farm

Secondary Content Role:

Dungeon

Balance Expectation:

Fast active-clear weapon combining burst and brief control. It should excel at dispatching ordinary enemies quickly while remaining useful, but not dominant, in longer dungeon or boss encounters.

---

## Dagger Pair

Gameplay Profile:

Combo / Execute

Primary Content Role:

Boss

Secondary Content Role:

Dungeon

Balance Expectation:

Dedicated single-target payoff weapon built around setup into assassination. It should rank among the strongest boss killers while giving up consistency and short-fight farming efficiency.

# Benchmark Rules

Weapon benchmarks must use the profile as interpretation context.

A benchmark report should answer two separate questions:

1. Does the weapon perform correctly inside its intended role?
2. Does it remain within acceptable global bounds outside that role?

Primary-role performance is the main balancing target.

Secondary-role performance is allowed to be competitive but should not usually dominate specialists.

Off-role performance must remain playable unless the weapon concept explicitly requires a sharper weakness.

No weapon should become the best answer to every content type.

---

# New Weapon Authoring Rule

When a new weapon is requested with a gameplay identity and/or content role, the AI must infer a coherent weapon package from that information before asking for additional specifications.

The AI should infer, where possible:

- expected combat cadence
- damage distribution between attacks and abilities
- likely cooldown structure
- setup or payoff mechanics
- control or sustain needs
- intended strengths
- intended weaknesses
- relevant benchmark scenarios

Questions are required only when an ambiguity would materially change the weapon identity or conflict with an existing game rule.

The default workflow is therefore:

Role and gameplay intent

↓

Infer strengths and weaknesses

↓

Design mechanics and cadence

↓

Author stats and abilities

↓

Benchmark inside intended role

↓

Validate global guardrails

---

# Data Ownership

Runtime combat systems must not depend on balance-profile metadata.

The canonical machine-readable assignments live in:

apps/client/src/data/weaponBalanceProfileCatalog.ts

These values exist for design tools, audits, benchmark selection and reporting.

Combat behavior remains owned by the normal weapon, ability and stat data.

---

# Immutable Design Rules

Rule 01

Every authored weapon specialization must have an explicit gameplay profile and primary content role before final balance validation.

Rule 02

Weapon profiles never grant hidden runtime bonuses.

Rule 03

A weapon is balanced first against the expectations of its primary role, then against global guardrails.

Rule 04

Weapons with different primary roles must not be judged solely by one universal DPS or Fame/hour ranking.

Rule 05

A weapon may be viable outside its primary role without being required to match a specialist there.

Rule 06

Future content roles may be added without changing combat architecture.

Rule 07

New weapon design begins from role and gameplay intent, not from copying another weapon's numbers.
