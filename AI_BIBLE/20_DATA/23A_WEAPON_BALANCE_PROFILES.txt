# 23A. WEAPON BALANCE PROFILES

---

# Purpose

Weapon Balance Profiles define what each weapon is intended to do well before numerical balancing begins.

They exist to prevent invalid comparisons between weapons with different purposes.

A weapon designed to kill bosses must not be considered weak simply because it produces less Fame per hour than a dedicated farming weapon.

A weapon designed for dungeon consistency must not be forced toward the same burst or single-target output as a boss killer.

Profiles are design and benchmark metadata only.

They never grant hidden combat bonuses.

---

# Family-First Constraint

Weapon identity is authored family first, specialization second.

The first two active abilities are shared by every specialization inside a weapon family. Therefore the family carries most of the gameplay identity and usually most of the natural content role.

A specialization signature ability may shift the family's center of gravity, but it should normally remain an adjacent variation rather than a completely different archetype.

Default rule:

Family Profile

↓

Dominant gameplay identity and natural content role

↓

Specialization Signature

↓

Minor or moderate shift in emphasis

A radical role change is allowed only as an explicit transformative exception when the signature ability genuinely changes how the weapon performs in content. It must never be assumed merely to create variety.

Examples:

- Bow family may naturally favor repeatable ranged clearing.
- Longbow may lean further into Fame farming and burst.
- Badon may redirect part of that same ranged foundation toward control and Dungeon value.
- Both must still feel and benchmark like members of the same Bow family.

Benchmarking must therefore check family coherence before treating each specialization as an unrelated archetype.

---

# Two-Axis Profile

Every weapon specialization is described through two complementary axes.

## Gameplay Profile

Describes how the weapon expresses its power.

Examples include:

- Bruiser / Execute
- Sustain / Burst
- Control / DPS
- DoT / Ramp-up
- Burst / Control
- Combo / Execute

This vocabulary is descriptive and extensible.

It is not a closed runtime enum that limits future weapon design.

## Content Role

Describes where the weapon is expected to create the most value.

Current useful roles include:

- General Progression
- Fame Farm
- Boss
- Dungeon

Future content may introduce new roles without changing the underlying principle.

Each weapon has one Primary Content Role and may have one Secondary Content Role.

The specialization role must remain coherent with its family unless a transformative signature exception has been deliberately authored.

---

# Core Balance Rule

Profiles describe expected performance; they do not directly modify performance.

There must never be logic such as:

- +X% damage against bosses because primaryRole = Boss
- +X% Fame because primaryRole = Fame Farm
- hidden dungeon multipliers derived from the profile

The role must emerge naturally from existing authored weapon data:

- base damage
- attack speed
- ability ratios
- cooldowns
- damage-over-time
- execute conditions
- control effects
- setup requirements
- handling and off-hand access

# Benchmark Rules

Weapon benchmarks must use the profile as interpretation context.

A benchmark report should answer three separate questions:

1. Does the specialization remain coherent with its family profile?
2. Does the weapon perform correctly inside its intended role?
3. Does it remain within acceptable global bounds outside that role?

Family coherence is checked before specialization optimization.

Primary-role performance is the main specialization balancing target.

Secondary-role performance is allowed to be competitive but should not usually dominate specialists.

Off-role performance must remain playable unless the weapon concept explicitly requires a sharper weakness.

No weapon should become the best answer to every content type.

---

# New Weapon Authoring Rule

When a new weapon is requested with a gameplay identity and/or content role, the AI must first resolve the owning family profile, then infer a coherent specialization package from that family plus the requested signature direction.

The AI should infer, where possible:

- expected combat cadence
- damage distribution between attacks and abilities
- likely cooldown structure
- setup or payoff mechanics
- control or sustain needs
- intended strengths
- intended weaknesses
- relevant benchmark scenarios

Questions are required only when an ambiguity would materially change the weapon identity, conflict with the family profile, or conflict with an existing game rule.

The default workflow is therefore:

Family identity and natural role

↓

Requested specialization intent

↓

Infer the allowed shift from the family baseline

↓

Design signature mechanics and cadence

↓

Author stats and abilities

↓

Benchmark family coherence

↓

Benchmark inside intended role

↓

Validate global guardrails

---

# Data Ownership

Runtime combat systems must not depend on balance-profile metadata.

The canonical machine-readable assignments live in:

apps/client/src/data/weaponBalanceProfileCatalog.ts

These values exist for design tools, audits, benchmark selection and reporting.

Combat behavior remains owned by the normal weapon, ability and stat data.

---

# Immutable Design Rules

Rule 01

Every authored weapon specialization must have an explicit gameplay profile and primary content role before final balance validation.

Rule 02

Weapon profiles never grant hidden runtime bonuses.

Rule 03

A weapon is balanced first against the expectations of its family and primary role, then against global guardrails.

Rule 04

Weapons with different primary roles must not be judged solely by one universal DPS or Fame/hour ranking.

Rule 05

A weapon may be viable outside its primary role without being required to match a specialist there.

Rule 06

Future content roles may be added without changing combat architecture.

Rule 07

New weapon design begins from family identity plus specialization intent, not from copying another weapon's numbers.

Rule 08

Specializations sharing the same family must not diverge radically by default because their shared abilities carry most of the gameplay identity.

Rule 09

A transformative specialization role shift requires an explicitly authored signature mechanic that genuinely changes content performance.
