# 23. EQUIPMENT DATA

---

# Purpose

The Equipment Data document defines every equippable item available in the game.

While every piece of equipment is an Item, not every Item is Equipment.

Equipment definitions describe gameplay properties that only apply when an item is equipped.

This includes:

- Equipment Slot
- Weapon Type
- Armor Type
- Equipment Statistics
- Passive Abilities
- Active Abilities
- Durability
- Repair Cost
- Set Membership

Equipment definitions never contain inventory information.

---

# Design Philosophy

The Equipment Data system follows six principles.

---

## Item First

Every Equipment definition references exactly one Item definition.

```
Item

↓

Equipment
```

Equipment never exists without an Item.

---

## Modular

Item identity and gameplay behavior remain separated.

Changing combat statistics must never require editing the Item definition.

---

## Data Driven

Every gameplay property is defined through data.

No weapon or armor type is hardcoded.

---

## Immutable

Equipment definitions never change during gameplay.

Only runtime durability and ownership change.

---

## Shared References

Combat, Loot, Vendors, Crafting and UI all reference Equipment using identifiers.

---

## Extensible

New equipment can be added without modifying existing definitions.

---

# Weapon Design Profiles

Weapon balance is defined by intent before raw numbers.

A weapon must not be designed or evaluated only by comparing its total damage with every other weapon in the game. Different weapons are allowed and expected to excel in different situations.

Weapon design uses two complementary profile axes:

1. Gameplay Profile
2. Content Role

These profiles are DESIGN AND BALANCE INTENT. They do not grant hidden runtime bonuses.

There must never be logic such as:

```
+20% damage versus bosses because role = Boss Killer
```

The role must emerge naturally from the weapon's authored statistics, attack cadence, abilities, cooldowns, effects and trade-offs.

## Gameplay Profile

The Gameplay Profile describes HOW the weapon creates value in combat.

Examples include, but are not limited to:

- Burst
- Sustain
- DoT / Ramp-up
- Combo
- Execute
- Control
- Bruiser

This vocabulary is intentionally open. New gameplay profiles may be introduced when a weapon fantasy requires them.

The profile should influence the construction of:

- base damage;
- attack speed;
- ability ratios;
- cooldowns;
- damage distribution between auto-attacks and abilities;
- DoT, execute, combo or control mechanics;
- reliability versus setup dependency;
- offensive versus defensive utility.

## Content Role

The Content Role describes WHERE the weapon is expected to create the most value.

Examples include, but are not limited to:

- Fame Farming / standard monster clearing;
- Boss killing;
- Dungeon progression;
- General progression;
- future authored content types.

A weapon normally has one Primary Content Role and may have one Secondary Content Role.

Content roles are not a closed enum at design level. As Albion Idle gains new content, new roles may be authored without redesigning the weapon architecture.

Typical expectations:

Fame Farming weapons tend to value short encounter efficiency, low downtime, reliable damage and fast repeated kills.

Boss weapons tend to value sustained single-target performance, setup payoff, execute windows or mechanics that retain value during long encounters.

Dungeon weapons tend to value repeated encounter consistency, versatility, control, survivability and/or the ability to handle different enemy patterns across a run.

These are guidelines, not mandatory templates. The exact mechanical expression must follow the weapon fantasy and existing combat systems.

## AI Weapon Creation Directive

When creating a new weapon, the AI should require only the design information that materially changes the weapon.

The preferred brief is:

```
Weapon identity / family
Gameplay profile
Primary content role
Optional secondary role or explicit special constraint
```

From this brief, the AI MUST infer the concrete implementation using the existing Albion Idle architecture and current balance baselines.

It should infer, where possible:

- appropriate attack cadence;
- relative auto-attack strength;
- ability structure and mechanical synergy;
- cooldown philosophy;
- expected strengths;
- expected weaknesses;
- suitable benchmark scenarios;
- which existing weapons are relevant comparisons.

The AI must NOT respond to every new weapon request with an exhaustive questionnaire.

Ask a clarification only when a missing answer would create a genuine design contradiction, violate an established system rule, or materially change the intended weapon identity.

If the role and gameplay intent are clear, proceed and make expert design decisions.

## Balance Construction Rule

A weapon's profile changes its TARGET DISTRIBUTION OF POWER, not the game's global power budget.

Creating a specialist weapon is not permission to simply increase its total power.

Strength in the primary role must normally be paid for through weaker performance, lower convenience, setup requirements, reduced control, reduced survivability, lower clear speed, lower sustained damage, or another meaningful opportunity cost outside that role.

The exact trade-off is chosen according to the weapon fantasy.

A weapon should have:

- an identifiable reason to equip it;
- at least one meaningful situation where another weapon is preferable;
- no expectation of universal parity in every metric;
- no expectation of universal superiority.

## Balance Evaluation and Benchmarks

Weapon benchmarking happens in two passes.

FIRST: Role validation.

Compare the weapon primarily against weapons that compete for the same content role or solve a similar gameplay problem.

Examples:

- a Boss Killer is evaluated primarily on boss-relevant performance;
- a Fame Farming weapon is evaluated primarily on repeated standard-monster efficiency;
- a Dungeon weapon is evaluated primarily across dungeon encounter patterns.

SECOND: Global guardrail validation.

The weapon is then tested outside its preferred role to ensure that specialization has not made it dominant everywhere.

Therefore:

```
Lower Fame/hour does NOT automatically mean a Boss weapon is underpowered.
Higher boss DPS does NOT automatically mean a Fame Farming weapon is underpowered.
```

The question is always:

```
Does this weapon fulfil its intended role at a healthy power level,
while preserving meaningful reasons to choose other weapons?
```

## Existing and Future Weapons

Existing weapons may be assigned profiles retrospectively without automatically changing their numbers.

Profile assignment alone is documentation and balance classification.

Numbers or mechanics are changed only when testing shows that the current weapon does not reflect its intended profile or violates global balance guardrails.

Future weapons should receive their gameplay profile and content role BEFORE detailed numeric tuning.

# Equipment Statistics

Equipment contains its base statistics.

Example:

```json
{
    "stats": {
        "stat_physical_damage": 128,
        "stat_attack_speed": 1.10
    }
}
```

Statistics reference Stat IDs.

---

# Passive Ability

Equipment may define one passive ability.

Example:

```
ability_passive_sword_deep_cuts
```

Passive abilities are optional.

---

# Active Ability

Equipment may define one active ability.

Example:

```
ability_sword_heroic_strike
```

Some equipment contains no active ability.

---

# Durability

Every equipment defines its maximum durability.

Example:

```
100

250

500
```

Runtime durability changes during gameplay.

Definitions never change.

---

# Repair Modifier

Defines the repair cost multiplier.

Example:

```
1.00

1.15

0.80
```

Final repair costs are calculated by the Repair System.

---

# Equipment Sets

Equipment may belong to a set.

Example:

```
set_soldier

set_guardian

set_assassin
```

Equipment without a set stores:

```
null
```

Set bonuses are handled separately.

---

# Quality Compatibility

Equipment supports Quality.

Quality is not stored here.

The Quality System applies modifiers dynamically.

---

# Enchantment Compatibility

Equipment supports Enchantments.

Equipment definitions never duplicate enchantment values.

Enchantments are calculated by the Enchantment System.

---

# Runtime Representation

```
Equipment Definition

↓

Equipment Instance

↓

Equipped Character
```

Instances store:

- Current Durability
- Quality
- Enchantment
- Owner

Definitions store none of these.

# Validation

Validation checks:

- Unique ID
- Valid Item Reference
- Valid Slot
- Valid Weapon Family
- Valid Armor Family
- Valid Statistics
- Existing Ability References
- Existing Set Reference
- Durability
- Repair Modifier

Invalid definitions never load.

---

# Unsupported Features

Version 1 does not support:

- Random Equipment Affixes
- Procedural Statistics
- Random Passive Abilities
- Rune Slots
- Gem Slots
- Equipment Evolution
- Dynamic Equipment Types

# Acceptance Criteria

The Equipment Data document is complete when:

- Every Equipment references one Item.
- Every Equipment has one slot.
- Weapon families are defined.
- Armor families are defined.
- Equipment statistics use Stat IDs.
- Passive abilities are optional.
- Active abilities are optional.
- Durability is defined.
- Repair modifiers are defined.
- Set membership is optional.
- Runtime instances remain separate.
- New weapons have an explicit or inferable Gameplay Profile.
- New weapons have a Primary Content Role before detailed numeric tuning.
- Weapon role labels do not create hidden combat bonuses.
- Balance benchmarks are interpreted according to intended role before global guardrail comparison.

Rule 01

Every Equipment references one Item.

Rule 02

Equipment belongs to exactly one slot.

Rule 03

Equipment statistics reference Stat IDs.

Rule 04

Weapon families are data-driven.

Rule 05

Armor families are data-driven.

Rule 06

One-Handed weapons allow Off-Hand equipment.

Rule 07

Two-Handed weapons disable the Off-Hand slot.

Rule 08

Passive abilities are optional.

Rule 09

Active abilities are optional.

Rule 10

Durability is defined per Equipment.

Rule 11

Repair modifiers are data-driven.

Rule 12

Set membership is optional.

Rule 13

Quality is handled by the Quality System.

Rule 14

Enchantments are handled by the Enchantment System.

Rule 15

Definitions remain immutable throughout gameplay.

Rule 16

Weapon Gameplay Profile and Content Role describe design intent and benchmark context; they never grant implicit runtime bonuses.

Rule 17

A new weapon is designed from role and gameplay intent before detailed damage numbers are tuned.

Rule 18

The AI must infer concrete weapon construction from clear role/gameplay intent using existing systems, and must not require an exhaustive questionnaire when the intent is sufficient.

Rule 19

Weapon balance is evaluated against role-relevant benchmarks first and global dominance guardrails second.

Rule 20

Weapon profiles and content roles are extensible design vocabulary, not a closed hardcoded runtime taxonomy.

---

# Summary

The Equipment Data document defines every equippable object in the game independently from inventory data and item identity. By separating gameplay behavior from Item definitions, it provides a scalable and modular architecture where combat statistics, abilities, durability and equipment sets remain entirely data-driven. Weapon profiles additionally define why and where a weapon should excel, allowing future weapons to be created and balanced from clear gameplay intent without forcing universal parity between fundamentally different roles. This separation allows hundreds of weapons and armor pieces to share consistent systems while remaining easy to balance, validate and extend.
