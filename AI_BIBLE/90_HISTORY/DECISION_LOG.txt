* what was decided;
* why it was decided;
* which alternatives were rejected;
* which consequences follow from the decision;
* whether the decision is locked or still open.

This document is not the primary specification of the game.

## ADR-GD-001 — Single-player-first game

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Project identity

### Context

The initial inspiration comes from Albion Online, a multiplayer MMORPG with a player-driven economy, guilds, PvP and trading.

The project needs a clear scope that can be developed and played without reproducing an MMO infrastructure.

### Decision

Albion Idle will be designed as a completely playable single-player game.

No core system may require:

* other players;
* guilds;
* PvP;
* trading;
* a player marketplace;
* social participation.

### Rationale

A single-player structure:

* reduces technical scope;
* enables deterministic progression;
* supports offline simulation;
* avoids dependency on an active player population;
* keeps the hero at the center of the game;
* makes the project feasible for an AI-assisted development workflow.

### Rejected alternatives

* Required multiplayer economy.
* Mandatory PvP.
* Guild-dependent progression.
* Simulated MMO as the initial objective.

### Consequences

All economic and progression systems must function without external players.

Optional social or multiplayer features may be considered later, but they cannot become a prerequisite for the core game.

---

## ADR-GD-002 — Idle RPG rather than economy simulator

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Project identity

### Context

Early discussions placed significant emphasis on gathering, refining, crafting, workers and economic progression.

This created a risk that the game would become primarily a production or economy simulator.

### Decision

The project is a:

> Single-player idle RPG inspired by Albion Online.

It is not primarily an economic simulation.

### Rationale

The player fantasy centers on a hero becoming strong enough to conquer increasingly dangerous regions.

Production systems are important, but they exist to prepare and improve the hero.

### Rejected alternatives

* Economic empire simulator.
* Worker-management game with an optional character.
* Marketplace-centered idle game.

### Consequences

Every major economic system must ultimately support:

* character power;
* equipment;
* consumables;
* exploration;
* combat progression.

A system that does not contribute to the hero or the adventure loop should be challenged before implementation.

## ADR-GD-003 — One persistent character

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Character

### Context

Some idle RPGs use multiple heroes, teams, alternate characters or class rosters.

This would increase complexity and dilute the progression identity of the main character.

### Decision

The player controls one persistent character.

There are:

* no alternate characters;
* no hero roster;
* no multiple classes;
* no party-management requirement.

### Rationale

A single character creates:

* stronger attachment;
* clearer long-term progression;
* simpler save data;
* easier mastery tracking;
* more meaningful equipment decisions;
* a direct relationship between player choices and character identity.

### Rejected alternatives

* Multiple character slots.
* Party-based idle combat.
* Character collection.
* Traditional class selection.

### Consequences

Build diversity must come from:

* weapons;
* armor;
* abilities;
* passives;
* equipment;
* consumables;
* masteries.

It must not depend on creating another character.

## ADR-GD-004 — No global character level

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Character progression

### Context

A traditional global level provides a simple numerical representation of progression but does not clearly represent what the character has learned.

A level 100 character using an unfamiliar weapon would create unclear progression logic.

### Decision

The character will not have a traditional visible global level.

Progression will be represented through specific masteries.

### Rationale

A mastery-based structure communicates character experience more accurately.

Examples:

* Sword Mastery;
* Bow Mastery;
* Mining Mastery;
* Skinning Mastery;
* Plate Armor Mastery;
* Sword Crafting Mastery.

The player improves through the activities they practice.

### Rejected alternatives

* Traditional character level.
* Level plus mandatory attribute allocation.
* Class level.
* Prestige or rebirth level.

### Consequences

The interface must clearly communicate overall progression without relying on a global level.

Any future aggregate score, such as a combat rating, must not function as a hidden traditional level unless explicitly validated.

---

## ADR-GD-005 — Use-based mastery progression

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Character progression

### Context

Without classes or a global character level, the game requires another system for permanent progression.

### Decision

The character progresses in the activities, weapon families, armor families and professions they use.

### Rationale

This reflects the progression philosophy associated with Albion Online while supporting a flexible single-character RPG.

The player’s history becomes visible through their masteries.

### Rejected alternatives

* Fixed class tree.
* Universal experience applied to any skill.
* Permanent class restrictions.
* Attribute-point allocation as the main progression system.

### Consequences

The mastery system must be modular and data-driven.

The exact list of masteries, their relationships and experience formulas remain to be defined.

---

## ADR-GD-006 — Hybrid character power

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Character progression

### Context

A purely mastery-based system could make equipment insufficiently important.

A purely equipment-based system could make long-term character progression feel temporary.

### Decision

Character power will use a hybrid model:

* masteries provide permanent progression and unlocks;
* equipment provides most immediate combat power.

### Rationale

This keeps both long-term progression and crafting relevant.

A player must develop expertise while still producing and maintaining appropriate equipment.

### Rejected alternatives

* Equipment-only power.
* Mastery-only power.
* Traditional attribute inflation.
* Permanent account bonuses that trivialize equipment tiers.

### Consequences

Balance must prevent either equipment or masteries from making the other irrelevant.

A high mastery should improve efficiency and options without automatically invalidating tier progression.

---

## ADR-GD-007 — Hero-centered systems

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Design philosophy

### Context

Gathering, refining, workers, buildings and island systems can easily become independent management games.

### Decision

All major systems exist to support the hero.

### Rationale

The core fantasy is adventure and character progression.

The player should understand why each production activity matters to their character.

### Rejected alternatives

* Independent worker economy.
* City builder as the primary game.
* Production systems optimized only for currency accumulation.
* Island management disconnected from combat progression.

### Consequences

Workers, buildings and island upgrades must provide clear value to the hero’s progression.

Their complexity must remain subordinate to the central RPG loop.

---

## ADR-GD-008 — Equal importance of combat and economy

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Core gameplay

### Context

The game combines adventure systems and production systems.

A strong imbalance could cause one half of the design to become secondary or unnecessary.

### Decision

The target conceptual balance is approximately:

* 50% combat and adventure;
* 50% gathering, refining, crafting and preparation.

### Rationale

Combat and economy should constantly feed each other.

Combat unlocks content and resources.

Production prepares the player for stronger combat.

### Rejected alternatives

* Primarily economic idle game.
* Primarily combat game with decorative crafting.
* Crafting as an optional side activity.
* Combat used only to generate generic currency.

### Consequences

The vertical slice must demonstrate both halves of the loop.

Neither progression path should allow the player to ignore the other permanently.

## ADR-GD-009 — Central gameplay loop

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Core gameplay

### Decision

The central loop is:

Explore
↓
Gather
↓
Refine
↓
Craft
↓
Equip
↓
Fight
↓
Loot
↓
Unlock new stages and regions
↓
Repeat

### Rationale

This loop directly connects economic preparation with adventure progression.

It ensures that collected resources have a purpose and that combat provides access to further production opportunities.

### Consequences

Features that do not support this loop require explicit justification.

The implementation may allow several activities to occur in different orders, but the macro relationship must remain intact.

## ADR-GD-010 — Adventure is the objective

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Core gameplay

### Context

A crafting-heavy idle game may encourage players to accumulate resources without a meaningful adventure objective.

### Decision

The economy is the means.

Adventure and progression are the objectives.

### Rationale

Resources, crafting and production become more meaningful when they prepare the player for a visible challenge.

### Consequences

The game should regularly present:

* new stages;
* stronger monsters;
* bosses;
* new regions;
* strategic challenges.

Currency accumulation alone must not be treated as the ultimate goal.

---

## ADR-GD-011 — Semi-idle combat

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Combat

### Context

Three broad combat models were considered:

* fully automatic combat;
* semi-idle combat;
* active RPG combat.

### Decision

Combat will be semi-idle.

Basic attacks and standard behavior occur automatically.

The player may intervene with limited strategic actions.

### Rationale

This structure preserves idle accessibility while giving the player meaningful control.

The player can optimize performance without being required to constantly interact.

### Rejected alternatives

* Completely passive combat without intervention.
* Real-time action combat requiring continuous attention.
* Large manually controlled ability rotations.

### Consequences

Manual actions should be useful but not mandatory for ordinary farming.

Hard content may reward intervention, but it must remain compatible with the idle identity.

---

## ADR-GD-012 — Weapon-based abilities

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Combat

### Context

The game requires combat abilities without introducing classes or a large universal spellbook.

### Decision

Combat abilities are primarily defined by the equipped weapon.

### Rationale

Weapons become strategic identities rather than simple stat containers.

Changing weapon changes:

* available abilities;
* combat rhythm;
* strengths;
* weaknesses;
* possible solutions to progression walls.

### Rejected alternatives

* Universal character spellbook.
* Class-based skill trees.
* Abilities permanently selected independently of equipment.

### Consequences

The ability system must be modular enough for each weapon family to define its own active and passive options.

---

## ADR-GD-013 — Two active abilities and one passive

**Status:** LOCKED AS CURRENT TARGET
**Category:** Combat

### Context

An early concept considered up to four active abilities.

This was considered potentially excessive for an idle RPG.

### Decision

The current fully developed weapon loadout target is:

* 2 active abilities;
* 1 passive ability.

### Rationale

The objective is to maximize meaningful choices rather than the number of buttons.

A smaller ability set is:

* easier to understand;
* easier to automate;
* easier to balance;
* compatible with mobile-like idle readability even if the target platform differs;
* sufficient for build diversity when combined with equipment and consumables.

### Rejected alternatives

* Four or more active abilities as the default.
* No active abilities.
* Large MMO-style ability bars.

### Consequences

This value may be refined through combat prototyping, but Claude should use it as the current design target.

---

## ADR-GD-014 — Progressive ability unlocking

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Combat progression

### Context

Providing every weapon ability immediately would weaken the feeling of specialization.

Restricting abilities only by tier would make mastery less meaningful.

### Decision

Weapon abilities will unlock progressively through a combination of:

* weapon tier;
* weapon mastery.

Higher-tier weapons may provide access to more usable ability slots.

Mastery may unlock new ability choices or activate additional slots.

### Rationale

The system creates two complementary forms of growth:

* acquiring better equipment;
* becoming more experienced with a weapon family.

### Rejected alternatives

* Every ability available immediately.
* Ability access based only on weapon tier.
* Ability access based only on global character level.
* Universal ability unlocks unrelated to weapon use.

### Consequences

The exact number of abilities, mastery thresholds and tier requirements remain open.

The implementation must keep these requirements data-driven.

---

## ADR-GD-015 — Minimal base statistics

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Combat and character systems

### Context

Traditional RPGs often rely on many attributes and derived statistics.

This can increase complexity without necessarily creating better decisions.

### Decision

The game will use a limited number of understandable base statistics.

Possible examples include:

* Health;
* Attack;
* Defense;
* Attack Speed.

### Rationale

Complexity should come from choices and synergies rather than opaque numerical systems.

### Rejected alternatives

* Large attribute sheets.
* Mandatory Strength, Dexterity and Intelligence structure.
* Numerous hidden derived statistics.

### Consequences

The final stat list remains open.

Any proposed statistic must have:

* a clear purpose;
* visible player impact;
* meaningful interaction with builds;
* sufficient value to justify its complexity.

## ADR-GD-016 — Five danger-zone families

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** World

### Decision

The world is divided into five major danger levels:

1. Blue Zone;
2. Yellow Zone;
3. Orange Zone;
4. Red Zone;
5. Black Zone.

### Rationale

The color structure provides:

* immediate readability;
* a clear long-term progression path;
* escalating risk;
* recognizable chapters;
* an intuitive relationship between danger and reward.

### Consequences

Each zone must feel like a substantial chapter rather than a simple resource tier.

The use of Albion-specific terminology will require an intellectual-property review before commercial release.

---

## ADR-GD-017 — Stage-based zone progression

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** World

### Context

An early concept proposed numerical subdivisions such as:

* 1.1;
* 1.2;
* 1.3;
* 5.1;
* 5.2.

### Decision

Each zone will contain multiple named or numbered stages.

Player-facing labels should use a format similar to:

* Blue Zone — Stage 1;
* Blue Zone — Stage 2;
* Yellow Zone — Stage 1.

Decimal stage notation will not be used.

### Rationale

A stage structure is:

* easier to understand;
* easier to expand;
* compatible with bosses, events and variants;
* less restrictive for future content additions.

### Rejected alternatives

* Decimal zone numbering.
* One continuous farming area per zone.
* Immediate access to all content in a color zone.

### Consequences

The exact stage count remains open.

Each color zone must contain enough progression to sustain slow optimization and preparation.

---

## ADR-GD-018 — Each zone is a progression chapter

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** World progression

### Context

Moving directly from one resource tier to another would make regions feel temporary and shallow.

### Decision

Each color zone must contain a complete progression arc involving:

* discovery;
* gathering;
* crafting;
* mastery progression;
* multiple stages;
* optimization;
* power checks;
* a zone boss;
* access to the next region.

### Rationale

The player should feel that a region has been conquered.

The next zone should be a meaningful reward.

### Consequences

A player obtaining the first item of a new tier must not automatically be ready for the next zone.

The zone should require build completion and optimization.

---

## ADR-GD-019 — General tier progression by zone

**Status:** LOCKED AS GENERAL DIRECTION
**Category:** World and equipment progression

### Decision

The early progression should broadly resemble:

* Blue Zone: Tier 3 and Tier 4 progression;
* late Blue Zone: Tier 4 optimization;
* Yellow Zone: Tier 5 access and progression;
* later zones: increasingly powerful tiers.

### Rationale

This creates a cycle in which each region introduces a tier and later demands optimization of that tier.

### Consequences

The final tier map remains open.

Tier access and zone access must not be treated as identical milestones.

---

## ADR-GD-020 — Progression requires preparation

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Progression philosophy

### Decision

A new zone must not become accessible solely because enough time has passed.

The player must demonstrate adequate preparation.

### Rationale

Without this rule, the optimal strategy would often be to wait.

The intended game rewards:

* build optimization;
* crafting;
* mastery planning;
* consumables;
* strategic adaptation.

### Consequences

Major progression gates should evaluate player readiness through content rather than arbitrary timers.

---

## ADR-GD-021 — Soft walls

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Progression

### Decision

The game will deliberately use soft progression walls.

A soft wall slows progression without making it impossible.

### Examples

* slow kills;
* inefficient gathering;
* poor resource yield;
* high repair costs;
* low farming profitability.

### Rationale

Soft walls signal that the player should optimize without completely stopping their activity.

### Consequences

Soft walls should be understandable through performance data and feedback.

---

## ADR-GD-022 — Hard walls

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Progression

### Decision

The game will deliberately use hard progression walls.

A hard wall blocks meaningful progression until the player improves or changes strategy.

### Examples

* an unbeatable boss;
* insufficient survivability;
* insufficient damage;
* failure to complete a required stage.

### Rationale

Hard walls create meaningful long-term goals and make zone completion satisfying.

### Consequences

Every hard wall must have identifiable and reasonable solutions.

It must not exist only to extend playtime.

---

## ADR-GD-023 — Knowledge walls

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Progression and combat

### Decision

Some challenges will function as knowledge walls.

The player may possess sufficient total power but use an unsuitable strategy.

### Examples

* wrong weapon;
* wrong ability;
* wrong passive;
* unsuitable armor;
* wrong consumable;
* misunderstanding an enemy mechanic.

### Rationale

The game should reward reflection more than blind grinding.

### Consequences

Combat feedback must help the player understand why they failed.

Knowledge walls must not depend on hidden information that cannot reasonably be discovered.

---

## ADR-GD-024 — Power checks

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Combat progression

### Decision

Important stages, mini-bosses and bosses may function as power checks.

### Possible checks

* damage;
* survival;
* sustain;
* speed;
* build composition;
* mastery;
* consumables;
* strategy.

### Rationale

Combat should prove whether the player is ready for the next progression step.

### Consequences

Different checks should encourage different improvements.

The answer should not always be more raw damage.

---

## ADR-GD-025 — Scaled death penalties

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Death and risk

### Decision

Death consequences increase with zone danger.

General direction:

* Blue Zone: no meaningful penalty;
* Yellow Zone: light penalty;
* Orange Zone: moderate penalty;
* Red Zone: severe penalty;
* Black Zone: very severe penalty.

### Rationale

The risk model should evolve with the player.

Early experimentation should be safe.

Late-game farming should require informed risk management.

### Consequences

Exact penalty values remain open.

Risk must be visible before the player begins an activity.

---

## ADR-GD-026 — Maximum durability loss instead of primary random trashing

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Death and equipment

### Context

An early concept proposed a random chance for equipped items to be destroyed after death in dangerous zones.

This could create severe frustration, especially during offline progression.

### Decision

Death primarily causes:

* current durability loss;
* gradual maximum durability loss.

Repeated deaths may eventually cause permanent equipment breakage.

### Rationale

This preserves meaningful risk while allowing the player to see equipment degradation approaching.

The player can choose to:

* repair;
* retreat;
* replace equipment;
* change farming location.

### Rejected alternatives

* Sudden random destruction as the primary death penalty.
* No meaningful late-game death penalty.
* Currency-only death penalties.

### Consequences

The permanent breakage threshold and repair rules remain open.

---

## ADR-GD-027 — Eight-hour base offline cap

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Offline progression

### Decision

The base offline progression duration is capped at eight hours.

### Rationale

Eight hours:

* rewards daily return;
* accommodates sleep or work periods;
* prevents unlimited passive accumulation;
* provides a clear progression upgrade opportunity.

### Consequences

The simulation must stop or limit rewards beyond the configured cap.

The exact calculation model remains to be defined.

---

## ADR-GD-028 — Expandable offline duration

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Offline progression and island

### Decision

The offline cap may be increased through permanent progression.

Possible sources include:

* island improvements;
* housing;
* beds;
* buildings;
* trophies.

### Rationale

Offline capacity becomes a meaningful long-term reward.

It also connects the island to the hero’s progression.

### Consequences

Possible future thresholds include 10, 12, 16 or 24 hours, but no values beyond the base eight hours are currently final.

---

## ADR-GD-029 — Offline progression executes prior preparation

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Offline progression

### Decision

Offline progression should execute a strategy configured by the player before leaving.

### Possible activities

* combat farming;
* gathering;
* refining;
* crafting;
* worker tasks.

### Rationale

Automation should carry out player decisions rather than independently decide the optimal strategy.

### Consequences

The player must have a clear way to select or configure the offline activity.

The exact activity list remains open.

---

## ADR-GD-030 — Frequent rewards, slow macro power

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Reward cadence

### Decision

Long-term power progression may be slow, but rewards must occur frequently.

### Rationale

Idle games require regular feedback and anticipation.

A long tier progression can remain satisfying when the player frequently obtains:

* mastery progress;
* loot;
* recipes;
* abilities;
* stages;
* enemies;
* upgrades;
* efficiency improvements.

### Consequences

Balancing must distinguish between:

* reward frequency;
* power growth speed.

These two concepts must not be treated as the same value.

## ADR-GD-031 — Data-driven content

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Technical architecture

### Decision

Gameplay content and balancing values should be stored as data whenever reasonable.

### Included content

* items;
* resources;
* recipes;
* monsters;
* bosses;
* stages;
* zones;
* loot;
* abilities;
* masteries;
* buildings;
* workers;
* events.

### Rationale

The project is expected to evolve through many balancing and content iterations.

Data-driven systems allow content to change without rewriting core code.

### Consequences

Claude must separate:

* system logic;
* content definitions;
* balancing values.

Temporary prototype values must not become hidden constants scattered through the codebase.

## ADR-GD-032 — Modular architecture

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Technical architecture

### Decision

Major game systems must remain modular and communicate through clear interfaces.

### Rationale

The project will be developed incrementally with AI assistance.

Modularity reduces regressions and allows systems to be replaced or extended.

### Consequences

The initial architecture should separate at least:

* character;
* inventory;
* items;
* equipment;
* masteries;
* abilities;
* monsters;
* combat;
* world;
* stages;
* loot;
* gathering;
* refining;
* crafting;
* save;
* offline simulation.

## ADR-GD-033 — Vertical slice before full production

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Production

### Decision

Development begins with a limited playable vertical slice focused primarily on the Blue Zone.

### Rationale

The project must first prove:

* that the core loop is enjoyable;
* that combat and production interact correctly;
* that progression walls are satisfying;
* that the architecture supports offline simulation;
* that Claude can extend the system safely.

### Rejected alternatives

* Designing and implementing all five zones before testing.
* Building complete workers and island systems before validating combat.
* Producing final art before the gameplay loop is proven.

### Consequences

Prototype and vertical-slice requirements must be clearly distinguished from future production features.

## ADR-GD-034 — Documentation follows implementation needs

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Documentation strategy

### Context

A traditional GDD is often organized by conceptual topic.

Claude requires specifications that can be translated directly into implementation steps.

### Decision

The documentation will be written in an order that supports development.

Each system document should distinguish:

* prototype requirements;
* vertical-slice requirements;
* later production requirements;
* future possibilities;
* dependencies;
* unresolved questions.

### Rationale

This reduces ambiguity and helps prevent Claude from overbuilding systems.

### Consequences

Documentation may not follow the order in which a player encounters systems.

The documentation order may change after the technical stack is selected.

## ADR-GD-036 — Intellectual-property caution

**Status:** LOCKED
**Category:** Production and legal risk

### Context

The project is explicitly inspired by Albion Online and currently uses Albion terminology during internal design.

### Decision

The project must avoid blindly copying protected assets, text, code, audio, visual designs or proprietary data.

### Rationale

A commercially released project must have an original identity and avoid avoidable intellectual-property risk.

### Consequences

Internal terminology and references are provisional from a commercial perspective.

A dedicated intellectual-property review will be required before public release.

## SUP-GD-003 — Decimal stage numbering

**Status:** SUPERSEDED
**Superseded by:** ADR-GD-017

### Previous direction

Stages could have been represented as:

* 1.1;
* 1.2;
* 1.3;
* 5.1;
* 5.2.

### Reason for change

A direct stage structure is clearer and easier to expand.

---

## SUP-GD-004 — Primary random item destruction on death

**Status:** SUPERSEDED
**Superseded by:** ADR-GD-026

### Previous direction

Items could have had a direct random chance to be trashed after death, with increasingly severe probabilities in dangerous zones.

### Reason for change

Sudden destruction could create disproportionate frustration, especially during offline farming.

Gradual maximum durability loss preserves risk while making failure more predictable.

---

## SUP-GD-005 — Four active weapon abilities

**Status:** SUPERSEDED AS DEFAULT TARGET
**Superseded by:** ADR-GD-013

### Previous direction

Higher-tier weapons could eventually support four active abilities.

### Reason for change

Four active abilities may create unnecessary complexity for a semi-idle RPG.

The current target is two active abilities and one passive.
